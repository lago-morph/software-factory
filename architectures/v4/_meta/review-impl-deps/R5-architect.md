# R5 — Adversarial architect review of `implementation-dependencies.md`

Persona: senior architect who knows v4 deeply and distrusts tidy narratives. Reviewing the current committed `architectures/v4/implementation-dependencies.md` against `_meta/component-inventory.md`, the v4 specs, README Part 4–6, and the review-log decision ledger.

Verdict at a glance: the doc's hard numbers (15/11, 19/22/25, ten-deep, thirteen-wide) all check out against sources, and the two load-bearing "spin" phrases (C41 "strongest principle match", C49 "riskiest invention leaf") are quotes from README/AI-CONTEXT, not invented. The real problems are (1) a genuine structural inconsistency in how C20 is modeled, (2) the "adopt + configure" framing under-stating authored work in a couple of places, and (3) silence on OPA that is *mostly* correct but leaves one fence sentence reading as more capable than the specs allow.

## Findings

### [MINOR] §opening (line 3) vs §backbone heading (line 24) — "fifteen" vs "eleven" — both correct but the bare opening line invites a contradiction read.
Line 3 says "one Gas City install brings up fifteen components at once"; line 24's bold heading says Gas City "brings up eleven of the twenty-five at once." Both are *true* — fifteen is the full Gas City delivery (C01–05, C06, C12, C13, C17–19, C23, C40, C41, C42 — verified, count = 15), eleven is the backbone subset. The doc does reconcile them (line 109 spells out "fifteen … eleven of those fifteen are in the backbone"). But the opening paragraph drops "fifteen" with no context 21 lines before the backbone says "eleven," so a reader hits the apparent conflict before the reconciliation. **Fix:** in line 3 write "fifteen components at once (eleven of them backbone)" so the first occurrence is self-disambiguating.

### [CONTRADICTION] §backbone diagram (line 30) + Spec-intake product row (line 46) vs §products catalog (line 90) — C20 is modeled two incompatible ways. — confusing and self-inconsistent.
The backbone folds C20 into "Spec intake (custom) … C08 C09 + bead schema C20" (line 30) and the backbone product table lists Spec intake as delivering "C08, C09, C20" as "one unit because … the bead types it produces are co-designed" (line 46). But the main products catalog gives **Bead-type schema its own separate product row** (line 90), delivering C20 alone, with a *different* dependency — "Gas City (bead store, C19)", not Spec intake. The inventory backs the catalog: `C20 → C19` (inventory line 32), and C20 is in the Persistence & Memory subsystem, not Spec Intake (inventory line 32 / §77 vs §81). So the backbone bundles C20 with C08/C09 while the catalog (correctly, per the inventory) treats it as a standalone product depending on C19. A reader cannot tell whether C20 is part of Spec intake or its own product. This is the exact "folding C20 into the Spec-intake node but giving it its own row" inconsistency. **Fix:** keep C20 as its own product everywhere; in the backbone diagram show "Spec intake (C08 C09)" and "Bead-type schema (C20)" as siblings both hanging off Gas City, and change line 46 to stop claiming C20 is delivered "as one unit" with C08/C09. The co-design rationale ("the bead types the bootstrap predicate reads") justifies an *edge*, not a *merge*.

### [MINOR] §products, Spec-intake row (line 89) — "Spec intake … C08, C09, C10, C11 … build from scratch" as one product — partly a forced bundle.
The catalog Spec-intake product lists C08 spec format, C09 prompt binding, C10 EARS linter, C11 intent crucible. C08→C09 is a real build chain (C09 depends on C08+C05). But C10 (EARS linter, depends C08) and C11 (crucible, depends C08) are independent leaves that share only "they read the spec artifact" — and the doc's own "top ten" treats C10 and C11 as separately-rankable deferred items (#3, #4, lines 261–262), i.e. *not* one build-test-integrate unit with C08/C09. Calling all four "one product" is defensible as a subsystem grouping but mildly oversells cohesion. **Fix:** acceptable as-is if a half-sentence notes C10/C11 are independently schedulable facets that need only C08, not the whole intake bundle (the top-ten table already implies this; make the product row consistent with it).

### [MINOR] §external products, self-heal split (lines 80, 96, 170) — splitting one capability across an adopted product (PyOD/HDBSCAN) and a custom product (diagnosis/fix) — honest but under-signposted at first encounter.
The self-heal chain C24→C36→C37→C38→C39 is split into "PyOD/HDBSCAN (adopt + compose)" for C36/C37 and "Self-heal diagnosis & fix (custom)" for C38/C39. This is *correct* and matches the inventory kinds (C36/C37 are library compositions; C38 is a Tracker-transfused agent, C39 a control-loop). The doc only states the split plainly at line 170 ("the self-heal capability spans two products"). At the product-table level (lines 80, 96) a reader sees two unrelated rows and must infer they're one capability. **Fix:** none required for correctness; optionally add "(self-heal, adopted half)" / "(self-heal, custom half)" labels so the seam is visible in the tables, not just in prose 90 lines later.

### [MINOR] §external products, "adopt + configure" framing (line 9, line 44) — honest for most, but "Inspect AI" and the C33 aggregator stretch it.
The four "kinds of work" verbs are well-chosen and the Inspect AI row is honestly labeled "adopt framework, then author" (not "adopt + configure"). Good. The one place the framing leans optimistic: C33 satisfaction aggregation is described in the eval-tier subsection as "Inspect AI score reduction + a small custom aggregator tool-node" (line 166) and is simultaneously "the hinge of the whole build" / "the most-depended-on node in the back half." Calling the hinge a "small custom aggregator" is the doc's only place where authored difficulty is verbally minimized on a critical-path node. **Fix:** drop "small" or qualify — the C33 spec (G09) treats the satisfaction-distribution definition as a non-trivial open design question, not a thin wrapper.

### [MAJOR] §Gas City product (lines 44, 109, 131) — "adopt + configure … brings up fifteen backbone components" — the G11 caveat IS stated, and stated well; but the headline framing still front-loads the tidy story.
The prompt asks whether "one install brings up fifteen" glosses authored work and whether G11 is buried. My finding: the doc actually handles this **better than the brief fears.** The G11 caveat is *not* buried — line 51 ("nobody has yet run `gc` end-to-end against v4's 'native' claims"), line 131 ("Every 'Gas City native' claim above is a claim to verify against a pinned `gc`, not yet a verified fact"), and the whole "the conformance check is the literal first practical step" framing are prominent and accurate against C01 spec §G11/AC-2 and inventory C01 gap G11. The "kind of work" is honest: line 117 carves out that C05's routing policy is "the only authored part," and line 129 explicitly defends why native capabilities are still separate verification gates. **The residual concern is narrower:** the headline verb is still "adopt + configure," and the bead-type *contract* (C20), the sling *routing policy* (C05), and the conformance *pack itself* (C01 AC-2, a transfused `conformance.go`) are real authored artifacts that the one-word verb hides — but the doc correctly externalizes each of these to its own product/row, so the Gas City row is legitimately "adopt + configure" once that authored work is accounted elsewhere. **Fix:** none required; this is defensible. Optionally add one clause to line 44's "Built as one unit because" cell noting the conformance pack is itself a small authored artifact, so "configure" isn't read as zero-code.

### [MINOR] §the fence (lines 48, 95, 201, 247) + the OPA question — silence on OPA is *correct*, but one dependency phrase over-claims enforcement strength. — cite C34/C43 specs.
The doc never mentions OPA. The specs are emphatic that **OPA was DROPPED** (C34 spec lines 39, 67, 126–129, 197–198, 213, 249, 253; C43 review RC43-07; SURVIVOR-PASS C42-06 "no OPA in scope"). So the fence's enforcement substrate is exactly what the doc says: "policy over Gas City process boundaries, rig partitions C42, and filesystem permissions" (lines 48, 95). **Silence on OPA is the right call** — naming a dropped technology would only confuse. One nuance the doc *omits* that the specs treat as load-bearing: C34's holdout enforcement is **detect-after-the-fact audit, not tool-call-time prevention** — the prevention residual is explicitly C43's job and is *aspirational until C44 twins land* (C34 spec §4.3, G21; C43 spec lines 168, 198 "aspirational until C44 ships"; XC-8). The doc presents C34 as "holdout integrity" and C43's boundary-typing half as a clean precondition without noting that, pre-twins, the holdout boundary is **audited, not enforced**. That's the single most important honesty caveat in the whole fence story and it's absent. **Fix:** add one clause to the fence subsection: "C34 *detects* holdout violations after the fact; tool-call-time *prevention* is C43's blast-radius bound and is aspirational until twins (C44) land (per C34 spec §4.3 / XC-8)." This is the fence's real residual risk and the doc currently reads cleaner than the specs warrant.

### [verified, no change] §rings — "drop C44 / C43 enters via its C42-only half" (lines 59, 201, 247) — fully faithful to D-20.
I pressure-tested the most suspicious move: the doc drops C44 from C43's dependency even though the inventory literally says `C43 → C42, C44` (inventory line 55). This is **not** a fudge — it is exactly the adopted operator decision **D-20** (review-log lines 52, 59, 305; C43 spec line 67): the boundary-typing/blast-radius half depends only on C42 and is a Phase-2 entry precondition; the twin-isolation half stays at Phase-3c with C44. The doc cites D-20's sibling D-20 reference (it cites D-20's content via "decision D-20" at line 59) and the split is the C43 spec's own binding design. Honest and well-sourced.

### [verified, no change] §C41 "strongest principle match" (line 126) and §C49 "riskiest invention leaf" (line 99) — both are quotes, not spin.
"The strongest principle match in the corpus" (C41) is verbatim from README P9 / AI-CONTEXT §3.1 ("Strongest match in entire corpus") and C41 spec line 12. "The riskiest invention leaf" (C49) is README line 278 "most significant invention" + component-inventory line 127 "Highest-risk leaf on the critical path" + C49 spec line 25. Neither is false-precision; both are faithful relays. *(One latent caveat the doc could note but isn't obligated to: C41's "strongest match" is conditional on Gas City *rejecting* unattributed writes vs merely *defaulting* the field — OQ-C41-3 / G36. Not a defect in this doc; just the same G11-class hedge that applies everywhere.)*

### [verified, no change] §critical path (line 243) ten-deep, §parallelism thirteen-wide (lines 12, 237), §rings 19/22/25 — all numerically grounded.
Traced the ten-component chain C01→C04→C28→C29→C32→C33→C46→C47→C48→C50 edge-by-edge against the inventory "Depends on" column: every edge is present, length is exactly 10. The 19→22→25 ring construction (closure of {C53,C43} + add C31 + drop C44, then +run-flow C05/C09/C18, then +safety C34/C41/C23) is internally consistent and each adjustment carries a sourced reason (C53 §AC-9 for C31; D-20 for C44). The "runnable=22 / safe=25" split is the doc's own synthesis but rests on cited acceptance criteria, not invented numbers.

## Verdict

**accept-with-named-amendments.**

Rationale: This is a strong, unusually honest document — the numbers are real, the "spin" phrases are sourced quotes rather than inventions, the G11 caveat is prominent (not buried, contra the brief's worry), and the C44 drop that looks like a fudge is actually a faithful rendering of adopted decision D-20. It is not a tidy narrative papering over engineering; it mostly resists that. But it is **not** accept-as-is, for two reasons that a knowledgeable reader will trip on:

1. **[CONTRADICTION] The C20 dual-model** — folded into Spec intake in the backbone, standalone product in the catalog, with conflicting dependencies (C08/C09 co-design vs C19). This is a genuine self-inconsistency that must be reconciled (keep C20 standalone everywhere).
2. **[MAJOR/MINOR] The fence's detect-vs-prevent omission** — silence on OPA is correct, but the doc presents holdout integrity (C34) and the boundary-typing fence (C43) as cleanly satisfied preconditions without the specs' load-bearing caveat that, pre-twins, the holdout boundary is *audited, not enforced* (C34 spec §4.3 / G21 / XC-8). One sentence fixes it.

The remaining items (15/11 first-mention disambiguation, "small aggregator" on the C33 hinge, self-heal split signposting, Spec-intake C10/C11 cohesion) are MINOR polish. Land amendments 1 and 2 and this is shippable.
