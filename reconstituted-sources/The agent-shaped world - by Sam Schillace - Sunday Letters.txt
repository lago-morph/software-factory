From my observations, there is a number of factors (simplification, evidently), with one being the most prominent.

Reasons for not trying new stuff:

* High comfort threshold

* Ignorance (wilful or forced)

* Barred access (lack of time, lack of resources)

* Strategic wait (let the stuff stabilise)

* Strategic spite (let their bubble pop)

Reasons to try new stuff:

* Curiosity

* Real needs or current incomfort

Reasons for trying and succeeding

* Overlap between tech and real needs

* High grit

* High intelligence and/or experience

* Ability to circumvent existing limitations

* Ability to detect and exploit value

I'd say most software engineers went through an active vibecoding phase with subpar results, myself included. That laid the basis for a strategic wait in optimistic personalities, or strategic spite in pessimistic ones. At some point when things aren't going well or when curiosity wins these engineers try again, with new knowledge and better toolset.

The ones that try again and are either sufficiently stubborn, sufficiently intelligent or sufficiently experienced strike the goldmine where most others fail. Handholding by someone with demonstratable results also works.

And there are others that either can't simply put understand / reeducate themselves to fit in the new world or really cling to the strategic spite despite all evidence. In some cases it's so blunt that it's almost like a self-preservation instinct that is failing to fire.
