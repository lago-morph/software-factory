# Discipline: Cognitive-escrow / interval-as-design-site

The interval between when an instruction leaves human possession and when its consequences return is a first-class design surface. The substrate fires *reflection prompts*, *success-criterion articulations*, *similar-past-prompt surfacings*, *delegation-level confirmations*, and (above threshold) a *STIR cascade* at the interval boundary — structurally, not voluntarily. The discipline operationalises F42 (Cognitive Escrow negligence, Kahana) and is one of the two corpus convergence-clusters that span both mandates (the other being bias-guard / opposing-side discipline, per D7-U-1's "two-things-both-true").

## Open questions

- **Does substrate-level promotion close F42, or merely surface it?** D7-U-1's "honest concession": opposing-side topology survives F53 structurally but does *not* close F42 at substrate; escrow-flavoured tracks are stronger there. The corpus signals *both* convergence clusters; the architecture set should likely carry both (D7-U-1 "Recommendation").
- **The operator's response to a substrate-fired prompt is itself voluntary.** U-B §7 OQ-PLEF-5: substrate can fire STIR but cannot make the operator engage with it. F53 may have a stronger reading than escrow-substrate addresses.
- **Interval granularity.** U-A §7 OQ-1 (*"the most load-bearing open question this track surfaces"*): at what cadence is an interval an `EscrowInterval`? Every send/receive pair? Every cycle? Every gate-boundary?
- **Escrow-threshold calibration.** U-B §7 OQ-PLEF-2: when does substrate transition STIR from mandatory-at-cold-start to offered-at-steady-state? Kahana doesn't say; corpus doesn't measure.
