## 1. Purpose & responsibility

C23 is **the append-only action ledger**: the run's durable, totally-ordered (per stream) record of *every action any actor takes*, written as JSONL with a monotonic `seq`, from which the audit trail (P9), the durable-workflow triggers (P10/P11 Orders, C40), and the lowest-impedance CXDB feed (C21/C24) are all derived. It is the **linearization point** of the factory: a thing "happened" once its event is sequenced and durable on C23, regardless of whether the richer content-addressed projection (C21) or the trace store (C27) has caught up.

Concretely C23 owns four capabilities:

1. **Durable append (DELTA-01).** `Append(stream, type, actor, payload) → EventRef`. The record is fsync-durable and assigned a monotonic, gap-free per-stream `seq` before the call returns. This is the commit point the rest of the system trusts.
2. **Ordered replay / tail (DELTA-03).** Consumers read forward from a durable cursor: `Read(stream, from_seq) → EventStream` and `Tail(stream) → live EventStream`. At-least-once delivery; ordering is the per-stream `seq`.
3. **Bounded back-pressure decoupling (DELTA-02).** Producers and consumers are decoupled by the durable log itself: a producer's `Append` never waits on a consumer; a stalled/dead consumer falls behind on its own cursor without blocking anyone or dropping records (it resumes from its last cursor).
4. **Attribution ledger (DELTA-06, P9).** Every record carries `created_by` (the `actor`), making C23 the canonical "who did what, in what order" audit substrate — the lowest-impedance, already-attributed, trajectory-shaped source for CXDB (AI-CONTEXT §5.4).

What C23 is **NOT**:
- **Not** the *content-addressed trajectory store* (C21). C23 is the flat, ordered, human-greppable JSONL ledger; C21 is the BLAKE3-dedup'd, branchable turn-DAG projection of it. AI-CONTEXT §5.5 enumerates exactly what CXDB adds *over* plain JSONL (dedup, O(1) branching, typed projection, perf contract, HTTP query) — those are C21's job, not C23's. C23 deliberately stays dumb, durable, and cheap.
- **Not** the *CXDB ingestion bridge* (C24). C24 is the standalone tool-node that *reads* C23 (or raw-API bodies) and *posts* to C21. C23 only exposes the read cursor; it does not know about CXDB.
- **Not** *messaging* (C06 Mail/Nudge). C23 is a fan-out *observation/audit* log (record-everything, many readers, replayable). Mail is durable point-to-point *coordination*; Nudge is ephemeral. C23 records *that* a mail was sent (an action), but is not the mail transport.
- **Not** the *bead work-graph* (C19) or *bead schema* (C20). C19 is the typed, mutable, queryable task graph; C23 is the immutable ordered stream of *changes* to it (and to everything else). C19 *emits* `BeadMutationEvent` to C23 (C19 DELTA-05); C23 stores no graph/status semantics.
- **Not** the *durable workflow engine* (C40 Orders). C40 *subscribes* to C23 to fire event-triggered workflows (crash/gate → Order). C23 is the trigger source; C40 owns the workflow execution + crash survival.
- **Not** an *OTel span store* (C25–C27). LangFuse/OTLP hold span-tree traces; C23 holds the flat action stream. Different shape, different consumer.

## 2. Context & dependencies

- **Depends on (inventory): C01** — Gas City substrate hosts the event bus as a native primitive (AI-CONTEXT §3.2 #3); C01 provides the storage mount/lifecycle (the JSONL segment files live on C01's mount, like C19/C21) and the `created_by` actor stamp via C41. C23 is a Gas City "always-on" concept (README P11: "Gas City event bus (**always**)"), so it has no Phase-gated install — it exists from Phase 0.
- **Foundational + Batch 1.** C23 is load-bearing for the whole persistence/observability/self-heal spine and is authored in the first wave (inventory "Batch 1"). Its envelope (DELTA-06) and delivery contract (DELTA-03) must freeze early because C19/C20/C21/C24/C40/C41 all build against it.
- **Producers (writers — "all actors", AI-CONTEXT §3.2 #3 P9):** **C19** (`BeadMutationEvent` per bead change, C19 DELTA-05), **C20** (`SchemaChangeEvent` on schema registration), **C28** (agent-loop actions), **C35** (override events), **C41** (every attributed action), and in principle every component that takes an action. The contract is *record every action*.
- **Consumers (readers):** **C24** (telemetry→CXDB bridge — reads C23 as the lowest-impedance CXDB source, AI-CONTEXT §5.4), **C21** (durable spool/replay source when CXDB is down — C21 DELTA-04), **C40** (Orders subscribe to fire event-triggered workflows), the **audit trail** (P9, README §228), and **C36/C57** (anomaly + failure-mode ledger may tail C23 directly for cheap numeric/audit signals).
- **Cross-cutting: C41** (identity/attribution) — C23 is *the* place `created_by` becomes an ordered, durable fact (P9); C41 and C23 co-own the attribution invariant.
- **Sits at:** the Persistence & Memory subsystem, one layer above C01, beside C19 (work-graph) and C21 (trajectory store). It is the *cheapest, most durable* of the three stores and the one the others fall back to (C21 DELTA-04).

## 3. Interfaces / contracts

Named-and-described (sweep 1; concrete JSONL line schema, cursor wire format, and conformance suite in sweep 2). The bus is offered behind an `EventBus` port so the Gas City native and a file-only fallback satisfy the same contract. *(Port justification: warranted only while it stays thin — the contract is small (append/read/cursor) — same G11 vendor-swap rationale as C21 DELTA-01; do NOT let the port re-implement Gas City internals — OQ1-class risk.)*

**Inbound — Append (producers: C19, C20, C28, C35, C41, all actors):**
- `Append(stream, type, actor, payload) → EventRef` — assign the next monotonic per-stream `seq`, wrap in the envelope (DELTA-06), append-and-fsync to the active segment, return `EventRef = {stream, seq, event_id}`. **Durability invariant (DELTA-01):** the record is on stable storage before return. **Ordering invariant:** `seq` is monotonic and gap-free within a stream. **Non-blocking invariant (DELTA-02):** `Append` latency is independent of any consumer's progress. Pre: `actor` (→ `created_by`) is non-null (P9); `stream` is valid. Post: record durable; `seq` assigned; idempotency key `event_id` stable.

**Inbound — Read / subscribe (consumers: C24, C21, C40, audit, C36/C57):**
- `Read(stream, from_seq, opts) → EventStream` — ordered forward read from a cursor; the replay primitive C21/C24 drain on recovery.
- `Tail(stream) → live EventStream` — follow new appends (the `Order`/bridge live path); resumes from a durable consumer cursor on reconnect.
- `Cursor(consumer_id) → seq` / `Commit(consumer_id, seq)` — **independent durable per-consumer cursors (DELTA-02/03):** each consumer tracks its own committed position so back-pressure is "consumer falls behind," never "producer blocks" or "record dropped." At-least-once: a consumer that crashes between processing and `Commit` re-reads from its last committed `seq` and dedups on `event_id`.

**Outbound (what C23 requires):**
- Storage lifecycle + mount from **C01** (segment files, fsync, supervised restart).
- The `actor`/`created_by` value from **C41** via C01 on every `Append`.

**Invariants:**
- **Append-only + immutable:** records are write-once; no update/delete of a record (segments are pruned whole, DELTA-05, never edited).
- **Durable-on-commit (DELTA-01):** `Append` returns only after fsync; this is why C21 can treat C23 as its durable fallback (C21 DELTA-04) and C40 can trust it as a crash-survivable trigger source.
- **Monotonic gap-free `seq` per stream (DELTA-01):** the linearization order; gaps would break replay-completeness for CXDB.
- **At-least-once + idempotent (DELTA-03):** delivery is at-least-once; `event_id = (stream, seq)` is the stable dedup key consumers (content-addressed C21, idempotent C24/C40) use. Resolves C19-OQ1 and C21-OQ2 in favor of "bus is at-least-once, dedup lives in consumers."
- **Producer/consumer decoupling (DELTA-02):** no producer blocks on a consumer; no consumer-side slowness loses a record — the durable log absorbs the lag (bounded by retention, DELTA-05).
- **Attribution-total (DELTA-06, P9):** every record carries non-null `created_by`.

## 4. Data model / state

C23 owns the *ordered, durable action log*; consumers own their cursors and their richer projections (C21 the turn-DAG, C19 the graph).

- **Record envelope (DELTA-06):** one JSONL line per event:
  `{ event_id, seq, ts, stream, type, actor /*=created_by*/, payload }` — `event_id = (stream, seq)` is the idempotency key; `type` is a **flat string** event name (`BeadMutationEvent`, `SchemaChangeEvent`, override, agent-action, …), **distinct from CXDB's `{bundle_id,type,version}` triple** (C23 is pre-CXDB plain JSONL). When C24 bridges a record into C21, this flat `type` is mapped to a CXDB type-triple at the **C24/C22 seam** (canonical bundle-id = the XC-4 integrator ruling); C23's `type` enum and CXDB's bundle namespace are not conflated. `payload` is the type-specific body. Versioned envelope so the line shape can evolve.
- **Stream (DELTA-04):** the partition unit. Per-`run_id` (and/or per-actor) streams, each its own append-only segmented log with its own monotonic `seq`. Avoids a global write lock and lets a run's events be replayed/pruned independently. A small set of well-known global streams (e.g. `factory.audit`) may exist for cross-run audit.
- **Segments + retention (DELTA-05):** each stream is a sequence of size/time-rolled segment files (`<stream>/<from_seq>.jsonl`) plus a durable per-stream head `seq` and a low-water-mark. Retention prunes whole segments below the low-water-mark by an age/reachability policy (e.g. "drained by all registered consumers AND older than N days"). This is the bounded-storage answer to "append-only forever."
- **Consumer cursors:** `{consumer_id, stream} → committed_seq`, durable, owned by C23 so a consumer restart resumes correctly (DELTA-02/03). The prune low-water-mark is **two-sided**: `max(min(committed_seq) over registered durable consumers, head − max_age_or_size_bound)` — normally the bus never prunes data a registered consumer like C24 hasn't ingested, **but a single dead/stuck consumer cannot pin the log past a hard age/size ceiling** (the DoS guard from DELTA-05). Crossing the ceiling raises a `consumer-lag` alarm and prunes anyway (a permanently-dead consumer loses un-ingested tail — the correct tradeoff vs. disk-fill stalling the whole factory).
- **Storage layout:** plain files on C01's mount (`events/<stream>/*.jsonl`, `cursors/`) — **no Postgres/Redis/Kafka** (mirrors AI-CONTEXT §5.3's "no Postgres/Redis/Kafka" posture for CXDB). Greppable JSONL is a feature (AI-CONTEXT §5.5 frames CXDB's HTTP query as the upgrade *over* grep — so grep-ability is C23's baseline value).
- **Consistency model:** an append is durable + ordered once fsync'd and assigned `seq`. C23 *is* the cross-component linearization point: C21 (DELTA-04) treats a turn as "real for the loop" once its source event is sequenced here. Single-writer per stream (DELTA-04) gives a total order without a global lock.

## 5. Behavior

Key flows (sweep 1 prose; sweep 2 = Mermaid sequence/state diagrams):

- **Append (happy path):** an actor (e.g. C19 after a bead write) calls `Append(stream=run_id, type=BeadMutationEvent, actor=created_by, payload)` → C23 assigns next `seq`, writes the envelope line, fsyncs the segment, returns `EventRef`. The producer is done; consumers catch up asynchronously.
- **Consume / bridge to CXDB (lowest-impedance path, AI-CONTEXT §5.4):** C24 `Tail(stream)` from its committed cursor → for each record, post to C21's ingest API → on success `Commit(consumer_id=cxdb-bridge, seq)`. Because the bus is at-least-once and C21 is append-idempotent (C21 DELTA-02), a re-delivered record is harmless. The bus's low-water-mark won't prune below the bridge's committed `seq`, so CXDB never misses events even after a bridge outage.
- **Back-pressure / consumer-down (DELTA-02, the G33 answer):** C24 (or C40, or LangFuse-feeder) stalls or crashes → its cursor stops advancing; producers keep appending; the segment log grows (bounded by retention headroom). When the consumer recovers it resumes from its committed cursor and drains the backlog in order. No producer ever blocked; no record dropped. If a consumer is *permanently* dead and not draining, the retention policy's max-age bound caps log growth and emits a `consumer-lag` health signal to C01 — a bounded, observable degradation rather than an unbounded disk fill or a silent stall.
- **CXDB-down fallback (interplay with C21 DELTA-04):** when C21 is unavailable, C24's posts fail and its cursor simply doesn't advance — the events are *already durable on C23*. C23 is the source of truth; C21 replays from C23 on recovery. This is why C23's DELTA-01 durability is load-bearing: C21's whole G33 degraded-mode story rests on C23 actually being durable, not best-effort.
- **Order trigger (C40):** C40 `Tail`s C23 for trigger event types (crash/gate); on a match it starts a durable Order. Because C23 is crash-survivable and replayable, an Order can be re-derived after a crash by replaying from C40's cursor (supports P10/P11 "survive crashes").
- **Cold start / restart:** C01 supervises the bus; on restart C23 reopens the active segment per stream, recovers the head `seq` (truncating any torn final line — see F-modes), and reloads consumer cursors. Producers resume appending at `head+1`; consumers resume from their committed cursors.

## 6. Failure modes & handling

| F-mode / gap | Risk | C23 handling |
|---|---|---|
| **G33 (major): no partial/cascading OSS-stack failure story** (relates) | A slow/dead consumer (CXDB bridge, LangFuse, an Order) could block producers or silently drop the audit/trajectory feed, cascading a stall across the run. | **DELTA-02 + DELTA-03 + DELTA-05:** producer/consumer decoupling via the durable log — `Append` never waits on a consumer; a stalled consumer falls behind on its own cursor and resumes; at-least-once + `event_id` dedup makes drain-after-outage safe; bounded retention + `consumer-lag` health signal caps the blast radius. C23 is the seam where downstream-down degrades to "consumer behind," not "factory stalled." It is also the *substrate* for C21's own G33 fallback (C21 DELTA-04). |
| **Producer crash mid-append** | A torn/partial final JSONL line on crash corrupts replay. | **DELTA-01:** append-then-fsync of a single complete line; on restart, recovery truncates a trailing torn line (it was never `seq`-assigned/returned, so no consumer saw it). Atomicity of the line + fsync barrier is the recovery contract (sweep-2 specifies the exact torn-write detection). |
| **Lost / duplicated event** (undermines CXDB completeness) | If events drop, CXDB trajectories are incomplete (the self-heal feed lies); if naively retried, duplicates corrupt counts. | **DELTA-03:** at-least-once delivery + stable `event_id` idempotency key → no loss (durable log + cursor low-water-mark) and harmless duplicates (consumers dedup). *Provides the mechanism* C19-OQ1 ("at-least-once vs drop") can be closed with, pending C19/integrator confirmation (C23 offers it, does not unilaterally decide C19's policy). |
| **Unbounded log growth** ("append-only forever") | Disk fills; v4 never says how JSONL is reclaimed. | **DELTA-05:** segmented logs + retention by `min(committed_seq)` low-water-mark and max-age; whole-segment pruning (never edit a record). Bounded storage with a durability floor that protects un-ingested data. |
| **F-mode P9: actor attribution / audit-trail integrity** | An action with no/forged actor breaks the audit trail (README P9). | **DELTA-06:** non-null `created_by` on every record (co-owned with C41); the ordered immutable log *is* the queryable audit trail (README §228). Signed provenance and the tamper-evident provenance hash-chain are C41's concern (G36, D-5); C23 guarantees the ordered, immutable, attributed record and the gap-free `event_id`s C41 chains over. |
| **F-mode P11: trigger reliability** | A missed/duplicated trigger leaves an Order un-fired or double-fired. | Durable ordered replay + per-consumer cursor (DELTA-02/03) lets C40 re-derive triggers after a crash exactly-once-effectively (at-least-once delivery + idempotent Order start). |
| **Global-bus contention / blast radius** | One global JSONL file = one write lock + one corruption blast radius for all runs. | **DELTA-04:** per-`run_id`/actor streams → single-writer-per-segment (no global lock), independent replay/prune, and a corrupt segment damages one stream, not the factory. |
| Clock skew on `ts` | `ts` used for ordering would be unreliable across hosts. | Ordering authority is the monotonic per-stream `seq` (DELTA-01), never wall-clock `ts`; `ts` is advisory metadata only. |

## 7. Cross-cutting

- **Security:** the immutable, attributed, ordered log is the P9 audit substrate; read access to a stream is a holdout/leakage surface (an actor must not tail a stream it isn't permitted to observe) — C23 honors C01/C42/C43 isolation on *who may `Read`/`Tail` which stream*. **Tamper-evidence ownership (D-5):** C23 provides **ordered gap-free `event_id`s only** and does **not** compute a provenance hash-chain; **C41 owns the provenance hash-chain, computed over C23's `event_id`s** (see OQ3, resolved). Signed actor provenance is likewise C41's (G36).
- **Cost/scale:** C23 is the *cheapest* store — plain fsync'd appends, no index, no hashing. Per-stream partitioning (DELTA-04) scales writers horizontally without a global lock. The cost lever vs CXDB: C23 keeps the durable ordered minimum; the expensive dedup/branch/query lives in C21, fed from C23. Per-stream throughput ceiling (single appender + fsync cadence) must be sized in sweep-2 against the aggregate action rate (every bead mutation + agent action + override).
- **Observability:** C23's own health — per-stream head `seq`, per-consumer lag (`head - committed_seq`), segment count, prune low-water-mark — surfaces to C01 `Health()` so the loop can *see* when a consumer (C24/C40) is falling behind (the bounded-degradation signal for G33). The bus is partly self-observing: a consumer-lag spike is itself an anomaly C36 can read off the bus.
- **Ops:** segment files back up as plain JSONL (no DB to operate, mirrors AI-CONTEXT §5.3). Two runtime contracts: bounded back-pressure (DELTA-02, continuous) and segment retention/prune (DELTA-05, scheduled). Recovery contract is torn-line truncation on restart (DELTA-01). `OTEL_LOG_RAW_API_BODIES` raw bodies remain a *separate* C24 path (AI-CONTEXT §5.4 "raw API bodies" row); C23 is the orthogonal "lowest-impedance event-bus" row — both can feed C21, and sweep-2 must state which is canonical per event class (OQ2).

## 8. Acceptance criteria & test strategy

1. **Durable append + ordering (DELTA-01):** N concurrent `Append`s to one stream produce gap-free monotonic `seq`; every returned `EventRef` is recoverable after a hard kill (`kill -9`) + restart (fsync barrier honored).
2. **Crash-torn-write recovery (DELTA-01):** kill the process mid-line-write → on restart the trailing torn line is truncated, the recovered head `seq` equals the last *returned* `EventRef`'s `seq`, and no consumer ever observed the torn record.
3. **Back-pressure decoupling (DELTA-02, G33):** freeze a consumer (C24 stub) → producers continue appending at unchanged latency (p99 `Append` independent of consumer state) and zero records are dropped; unfreeze → consumer drains the full backlog in order from its committed cursor.
4. **At-least-once + idempotent replay (DELTA-03):** force re-delivery of already-processed records (consumer crash between process and `Commit`) → the consumer re-reads from its committed `seq`, dedups on `event_id`, and the downstream projection (C21 stub) has zero duplicate effects.
5. **Retention floor protects un-ingested data (DELTA-05):** with the CXDB-bridge consumer stopped, the prune pass does NOT prune segments below the bridge's committed `seq` (low-water-mark honored); a `consumer-lag` health signal is emitted; restarting the bridge drains and then allows pruning.
6. **Per-stream isolation (DELTA-04):** corrupt one stream's segment → other streams `Append`/`Read` unaffected; per-stream `seq` independent; no global lock observable under concurrent multi-stream load.
7. **Attribution-total (DELTA-06, P9):** every record has non-null `created_by`; an `Append` with a null actor is rejected; the audit `Read(factory.audit)` reconstructs "who did what in what order."
8. **Port swappability:** a file-only `EventBus` implementation and the Gas City native pass the same conformance suite, proving C19/C20/C24/C40 code against the port, not Gas City internals.
9. **CXDB-fallback interplay (with C21 DELTA-04):** kill C21 → C24 cursor stalls but events stay durable on C23; restart C21 → C24 replays from C23 with zero loss and zero duplicate turns (joint test with C21 acceptance #4).

## 9. Open questions

- **OQ1 (→ review-log, top):** Stream-partitioning granularity (DELTA-04) — per-`run_id`, per-actor, or both? Too-fine = many tiny segment files + cursor bookkeeping; too-coarse = write contention + larger blast radius. The choice sets the C40 trigger-subscription model and C24's drain parallelism, and interacts with C01's mount/lifecycle. Needs a sizing pass against the expected action rate.
- **OQ2:** Canonical CXDB feed per event class — AI-CONTEXT §5.4 ranks the C23 event-bus path *highest* (lowest impedance) but *recommends* the raw-API-bodies path (C24/C25). Which event classes flow C23→C21 vs raw-bodies→C21? If both, what dedups them at C21? Co-spec with C24 (G26/G27) and C21 (OQ2 there).
- **OQ3 (→ RESOLVED by D-5):** Tamper-evidence beyond append-only-on-disk. The integrator's ruling **D-5** assigns the provenance hash-chain to **C41**, computed over **C23-provided ordered gap-free `event_id`s**. C23 therefore does **not** hash-chain its own records; it guarantees only the ordered, gap-free, durable `event_id` sequence that C41's `verify_chain` keys on (plus C21's BLAKE3 as the content-addressed tamper-evident copy on ingest). Residual: the un-ingested window before C41 chains / C21 ingests is bounded by retention, not by a C23-side chain — accepted per D-5.
- **OQ4 (→ G33 residual):** The `consumer-lag` max-age retention bound (DELTA-05) trades durability for bounded disk: if a consumer is down *longer* than max-age, C23 must either block pruning (risk disk fill) or prune ingested-but-not-by-this-consumer data (risk loss). The policy knob and its escalation (page the operator vs degrade) needs a decision tied to the autonomy-ladder (C56) and the failure-mode register (C57).
- **OQ5:** Whether the per-consumer cursor store (DELTA-02/03) is owned by C23 or delegated to each consumer. C23-owned gives a single low-water-mark authority for pruning; consumer-owned is simpler but makes the prune low-water-mark a distributed query. Determines the C23↔C24↔C40 cursor seam.
