## Part 1 — what happened

### Phase 1 — Reframe the build order around "minimum to safe self-build"

**Goal.** The operator asked for the most aggressive build order that gets the factory to where it can safely build itself, then the top-ten next components by cost/benefit — and to make that the lead of [`implementation-dependencies.md`](../architectures/v4/implementation-dependencies.md), with dotted-line soft dependencies and an answer to "why are beads a separate component when Gas City needs them to run?"

**Approach + what actually happened.** I read the inventory, the C01/C19 specs, and the two human-facing guides, then computed the dependency closure of the self-build milestone `{C53, C43}` with a script: 19 strict → 22 run-flow → 25 safety-collar. The key insight, which the existing one-line "long pole" had missed, is that the inventory's "Depends on" column records *spec* dependencies, not *runtime* ones — so the strict closure omitted the components needed to actually *run* a build (C05/C09/C18) and, as an adversarial subagent caught, the scenario runner C31. Per the project's standing rule I dispatched **four real subagents** (2 adversarial, 2 cooperative) to pressure-test completeness, the cost/benefit ranking, and the beads/Gas-City framing.

**The two corrections that mattered.** The adversaries surfaced two structure-changing claims, both of which I verified against primary sources before acting: (1) **C31 scenario-runner is required** and was missing (`spec/C53` AC-9: "composes C30/C31/C32/C33 + C51"); (2) **decision D-20 splits C43** so only the boundary-typing half is the precondition and the twin half **C44 defers** (`panel/VERDICT.md` line 14). The net was a C44→C31 swap that kept the backbone at 25. A second adversary corrected my false claim that CXDB (C21) is an "unsolved invention" — it is adopted upstream OSS; the unsolved piece is the C49 replay *driver*.

### Phase 2 — Incorporate live operator feedback into clusters + a product table

**Goal + what happened.** Mid-task the operator sent a sequence of refinements: group what's implemented together; "install and configure Gas City picks up many components at once"; distinguish install/configure from glue logic; make it concrete with examples; and finally — "it should be a table of clusters, each row a *product name* and a list of components." Each refinement folded into the same evolving doc rather than spawning a separate task. The terminal form is a `Product → components → adopt-vs-build` table (Gas City, Claude Code, CXDB, Inspect AI, OTel+LangFuse, PyOD/HDBSCAN, DSPy/Optuna/Unleash, LocalStack, OPA, + a custom row) that makes "one Gas City adoption discharges eleven backbone components" legible. The beads confusion became a standing section, "Why several components are really one install," which also exposed and fixed a real framing weakness.

**A bug caught by tooling.** The C44→C31 swap was net-zero on count, but an earlier slip had seeded "26" in eight places; a scripted recount of the cluster union plus a grep fixed all eight. The internal-refs checker also flagged bare-text/backtick-path issues, which I reworded clean even though it is not CI-gated.

### Phase 3 — Merge, then refresh the stale handoff, then this retro

**Goal + what happened.** On instruction I merged PR #224, then discovered the operator was right that `_meta/HANDOFF.md` was stale (frozen at the 2026-05-31 Sweep-1 close). It predated the entire wrap-up run: operator decisions D-20…D-25, the panel VERDICT, the three guides, and the backbone. ## Part 3 — agents-file suggestions

### Suggestion 1: Functional closure beyond the dependency edge-list — `AGENTS-MD-ad954598ce`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-ad954598ce-functional-closure-beyond-edge-list.md](./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-ad954598ce-functional-closure-beyond-edge-list.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Functional closure beyond the dependency edge-list.** "When computing a build order or minimal backbone from a dependency table, do not stop at the transitive closure of the edge column — walk the actual runtime story of the target capability and add the components that are functionally required to *run* it but that no edge names. Present the result as explicit rings (strict-closure / runnable / safe) so the gap between the graph and reality is visible, not hidden."
>
> *Grounded in: the v4 self-build backbone, where the inventory's Depends-on closure omitted the scenario runner C31 and the entire run-flow (C05/C09/C18).*

**Justification:**

The "Depends on" column records what a component's *spec* references, not what it takes to *run* the capability. The strict closure of `{C53, C43}` produced a 19-component set that could not actually execute a build — no dispatch, no prompt binding, and (caught only by an adversary) no scenario runner to feed the judge. The marginal cost is one "narrate the runtime story and diff against the closure" pass; the cost of skipping it is a backbone that cannot reach its own milestone, discovered only at run time. The ring presentation (19→22→25) makes the graph-vs-reality gap an explicit line item.

### Suggestion 2: Verify a subagent's structure-changing claim against its cited source — `AGENTS-MD-e761c18c8e`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-e761c18c8e-verify-subagent-structural-claim.md](./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-e761c18c8e-verify-subagent-structural-claim.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Verify a subagent's structure-changing claim against its cited source.** "Before integrating a subagent finding that changes a deliverable's structure — adds or removes a node in a graph, flips a binding decision, swaps membership — open the primary source it cites and confirm it, rather than trusting the subagent's summary. Do this even when the subagent is one you dispatched and trust."
>
> *Grounded in: an adversarial reviewer's claims that C31 was required and that decision D-20 splits C43 (deferring C44) — both materially changed the backbone and both were verified against spec/C53 and panel/VERDICT.md before acting.*

**Justification:**

Two adversarial findings each changed the backbone's membership; both were correct, but acting on a wrong one would have shipped a wrong graph into a doc the next run treats as the spine. Opening the cited sources cost two reads and converted "a subagent said so" into "the source says so, quoted." A structural change is exactly where subagent summary-compression is most expensive to get wrong: one verification read versus a corrupted graph propagating into every downstream plan.

### Suggestion 3: Recompute counts after membership changes — `AGENTS-MD-83adbc19ff`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-83adbc19ff-recompute-counts-after-membership-change.md](./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-83adbc19ff-recompute-counts-after-membership-change.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Recompute counts after membership changes; never trust a carried-over total.** "When the membership of a counted set changes during editing — even a one-in-one-out swap that 'should' leave the total unchanged — recompute the count programmatically and grep every stated instance of it in the document before committing. Stated totals drift silently across edits."
>
> *Grounded in: swapping C44 out for C31 kept the backbone at 25, but the draft asserted 26 in eight places until a scripted recount caught it.*

**Justification:**

The swap was net-zero on count, so the instinct was to leave the numbers — exactly wrong, since an earlier slip had already seeded "26" in eight places. A three-line recount plus a grep fixed all eight in one pass. One script run per membership change versus a document that contradicts itself on its central figure. Counts in a long doc are the most drift-prone fact; mechanize their check.

### Suggestion 4: Promote user conceptual confusion into the artifact — `AGENTS-MD-7b9acf7cca`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-7b9acf7cca-promote-user-confusion-into-artifact.md](./2026-06-01-225/AGENTS-MD-7b9acf7cca-promote-user-confusion-into-artifact.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Promote user conceptual confusion into the artifact, not just the chat reply.** "When the user expresses confusion about how a deliverable models something, treat it as a defect in the deliverable: add or rewrite a section that answers it durably, then mirror the answer in chat. A confusion voiced once is usually shared by every future reader of the artifact."
>
> *Grounded in: the user's 'why are beads separate from Gas City' question, which became the permanent doc section 'Why several components are really one install' and exposed a real framing weakness.*

**Justification:**

Answering only in chat would have left the same confusion latent for every future reader — and missed that the question exposed a real framing weakness (capabilities shipping inside one binary presented as independent boxes). Converting the answer into a standing section fixed the artifact and seeded the product→components table the operator then asked for. One section instead of one chat paragraph, and the artifact now pre-empts the confusion for everyone. User confusion is free QA signal about the deliverable — spend it on the deliverable.
