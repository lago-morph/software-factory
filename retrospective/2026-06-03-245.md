Proposed additions to `AGENTS.md`. ### Suggestion 1: Lead freezes the shared cross-component contract as a ledger decision before the dependent fanout — `AGENTS-MD-e87d8ed027`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-e87d8ed027-lead-freezes-contract-before-fanout.md](./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-e87d8ed027-lead-freezes-contract-before-fanout.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Lead freezes the shared cross-component contract as a ledger decision before the dependent fanout.** When a parallel authoring fanout shares one cross-component contract (a record schema, an interface, a payload), the lead agent MUST design and freeze that contract as a numbered ledger decision FIRST, then ship its verbatim text in every dependent subagent's brief. The keystone-builder-first pass then becomes a verification step (does the contract fit the owning component?), not the contract-discovery step. Do not let each subagent infer the shared contract independently.
>
> *Grounded in: D-43 frozen before the C32 keystone + the C52/C53/C30/C08/C34 wave; the seam adversary found zero drift on the lead-frozen records.*

**Justification:**

The triangle-eval plan said "C32 first because it defines the diagnosis contract" — i.e. the contract would be *discovered* by authoring the keystone. Instead the lead designed `DiagnosisRecord` up front and froze it as **D-43**, shipping its verbatim field table to all six subagents. The payoff was measured: the seam adversary field-level-verified `ScoreRecord` and `DiagnosisRecord` against every consumer and found **zero drift**. The contrast in the same session is stark — the one contract *not* lead-frozen (the correction-request payload, each side authored independently) drifted on three axes and needed a full integration pass to fix. Marginal cost: one ledger entry before dispatch. Cost of skipping: a whole adversary-find-plus-integrator cycle per shared contract.

### Suggestion 2: Seam adversary verifies producer-against-consumer field-by-field — `AGENTS-MD-f6dad320f8`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-f6dad320f8-seam-adversary-producer-vs-consumer.md](./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-f6dad320f8-seam-adversary-producer-vs-consumer.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Seam adversary verifies producer-against-consumer field-by-field, not only consumer-against-contract.** A field-level seam review MUST compare the producer's emitted payload against each consumer's declared inbound schema directly, field name by field name — not merely check each side against the frozen contract. Producer and consumer payloads authored by separate parallel subagents drift on field names, traceability keys, and enum sets even when each side is internally correct and cites the same contract.
>
> *Grounded in: the correction-request seam — C52 (producer), C08 + C30 (consumers) each internally correct but three divergent schemas; the frozen records they all cited were clean.*

**Justification:**

The seam adversary was briefed to verify the two frozen records and they were clean — because they were lead-frozen. Its one real finding was a contract with *no single owner*: C52 → C08 + C30, each authoring its own end. All three were internally coherent and cited the same decisions, yet the producer emitted `{diagnosis_ref, defect_summary}`, C08 expected `{factory_build_ref, defect_detail, requested_by}`, C30 expected `{factory_build_ref, rationale, correction_mode}` — three rationale names, two traceability keys, a `requested_by` the producer never set. A consumer-against-contract check passes each side; only a producer-against-consumer diff catches it. This is the prior run's `score_value` blocker class. Marginal cost: one extra column in the verification table.

### Suggestion 3: Scope heuristic-linter checks to changed files and the actionable class — `AGENTS-MD-0a5afdd186`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-0a5afdd186-scope-heuristic-linter-to-changed-files.md](./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-0a5afdd186-scope-heuristic-linter-to-changed-files.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Scope heuristic-linter output to your changed files and the actionable finding class before declaring done.** When a repo-wide heuristic checker reports a large pre-existing backlog (hundreds or thousands of findings across files you never touched), do not treat a nonzero exit as a blocker and do not fix the noise. Filter the output to the files your change touched AND to the high-signal finding class (e.g. BROKEN_LINK over heuristic BARE_TEXT/BACKTICK_PATH guesses), fix only what your change introduced, and verify that subset is clean.
>
> *Grounded in: check-internal-refs.py reporting 4627 repo-wide issues, of which exactly one BROKEN_LINK was introduced by this change.*

**Justification:**

`check-internal-refs.py` exited 1 with **4627 findings across 672 files** — mostly pre-existing heuristic false-positives (it flags the bare word "spec" in prose and every backtick config path). Taken at face value a nonzero exit looks like a failing gate; chasing it would mean editing hundreds of untouched files. Two filters — changed-files + the `BROKEN_LINK` class — isolated exactly one real bug (a wrong relative-path depth to ADR-0069 in `auto-002`). The scoped check is two greps; the unscoped reaction either blocks a clean PR on a 4627-issue backlog or drags it into an unrelated change.

### Suggestion 4: Commit each background subagent's disjoint files at its completion, not on the stop-hook nag — `AGENTS-MD-a5771bc308`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-a5771bc308-commit-per-completed-subagent.md](./2026-06-03-245/AGENTS-MD-a5771bc308-commit-per-completed-subagent.md)

**Proposed agent instruction:**

> **Commit each background subagent's disjoint files at its completion; treat the stop-hook's mid-wave uncommitted-changes warning as expected.** In a parallel background-subagent orchestration where the orchestrator owns all git, commit each subagent's disjoint output as that subagent reports completion — never mid-write, and never let the stop-hook's uncommitted-changes nag (which fires on in-flight subagent writes) push you into committing a half-written wave. The orchestrator's commit cadence is per-completed-subagent, not per-hook-fire.
>
> *Grounded in: the Wave-2 stop-hook nags while five background subagents were still writing C52/C53/C30/C08/C34.*

**Justification:**

During Wave 2 the stop-hook fired "uncommitted changes" on essentially every turn-boundary while five background subagents were still writing. The signal is real but its timing is misleading in a fan-out: at any mid-wave instant the tree holds several agents' partial output, and committing then captures half-written files and races the agents' writes. The discipline that worked: commit each subagent's two files the moment its receipt arrives (disjoint paths, no index race), and read the nag as "a wave is in flight," not "commit now." Cost of the wrong reaction: a corrupt half-file commit; cost of the right one: tolerating an expected warning.
