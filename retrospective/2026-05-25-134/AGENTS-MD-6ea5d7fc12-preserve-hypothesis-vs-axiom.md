# agent instruction

**Preserve "hypothesis, not axiom" framing verbatim.** When the user marks a position as a hypothesis (falsifiable, subject to empirical override), preserve that framing verbatim in every document that records it. Do not soften "hypothesis" to "principle", "framework", or "orientation"; do not promote it to an axiom by omitting the qualifier. The falsifiability marker is load-bearing — it determines whether downstream pressure-testing can overturn the claim.

# justification

In PR #134's DEC-1 resolution, the user confirmed the working hypothesis "no methodology serves both mandates; substrates and disciplines do" — and explicitly added the parenthetical "(make sure to keep it as a hypothesis, not an axiom)". They reinforced the same point twice more in follow-up messages, marking within-greenfield claims as *hypothesis, NOT axiom* (capitals theirs). The reason is structural: the v3 synthesis's scoping principle ("carry all defensible candidates") and Phase-8 lean-eval design both depend on candidates being able to *falsify* the orienting hypothesis. If "hypothesis" silently becomes "principle" in resolved-decisions docs, the empirical override path disappears, and what was meant to be a starting frame becomes an unfalsifiable commitment.

Marginal cost of preserving the framing: zero — it's just a word. Cost of softening it: structurally, the synthesis loses its mechanism for catching the case where the hypothesis is wrong; doctrinally, the next agent reads a "principle" as binding and may not even consider falsification evidence when it arrives. The user has now flagged this preference twice in two sessions; the rule is overdue.
