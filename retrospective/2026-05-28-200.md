## Part 1 — What happened (factual)

This run was the final v3 synthesis phase. Phase 8 delivers per-candidate lean-eval brief designs + a cross-candidate evaluator-brief that pre-commits the DEC-1.a falsifying result pattern before downstream simulator-harness execution. The run produced 10 lean-eval briefs (1 lead-agent exemplar GF-M + 9 sibling subagent-authored), 3 cross-candidate bias-guard audits (domain-practitioner + falsification-designer + hypothesis-falsifier per the A.2′ shape that auto-008 Round 2 selected over Round 1's A.1 per-candidate-paired shape), a lead-agent cross-check artifact, and a Phase-8-close handoff that names the v3 synthesis pipeline as COMPLETE.

The auto-008 brief went through Round 1 + Round 2 of real adversarial review (6 subagents across 3+3 angles): Round 1 dispatched pre-mortemer + falsification-designer + cost-hawk; Round 2 dispatched regulator + 10-year-on-call-engineer + cross-mandate-attacker. All 6 returned `accept-with-named-amendments`. **The cross-mandate-attacker reviewer in Round 2 found the load-bearing structural defect**: the DEC-1.a falsifying pattern in Round-1 said "≥2 unified-attempts pass cleanly on BOTH the greenfield-mandate lean-eval AND the brownfield-mandate lean-eval", but the file model has only ONE lean-eval brief per unified-attempt candidate. Round-2 amendments fixed this by partitioning the §1 scenario set into `### Greenfield-mandate scenarios` + `### Brownfield-mandate scenarios` subsections within each unified-attempt brief + redefining "pass cleanly" per-bloc + adding the R6 #5 structural rider preventing scope-claim escape-hatches. All 4 unified-attempts (U-A, U-B, U-C, D7-U-1) honored this discipline (3 GF + 3 BF each).

The domain-practitioner audit in Wave 8.1.b surfaced a separate load-bearing finding: U-A, U-B, D7-U-1 falsifiers are **mechanically-sound** (pass the falsification-designer's 4-item rubric) but **practitioner-thin** — they measure substrate-emitted distributions/confidences/counts rather than software-quality outcomes a practitioner would care about. The cross-candidate evaluator-brief absorbed this finding via a two-tier "pass cleanly" report (mechanical vs practitioner) + a DEC-1.a falsification strength gradient (strong / mechanical-only / not-falsified).

The hypothesis-falsifier audit named the DEC-1.a falsifying result pattern verbatim and committed it to disk BEFORE the simulator-harness executes the lean-evals. The R2 #4 honest-framing amendment ("named before simulator runs but after brief authoring") was honored: the audit's first section states explicitly how the auditor guarded against fitting the observed pattern (canonical-primitive constraint: falsifier composed only from terms committed in auto-008 BEFORE per-candidate briefs were authored).

Two notable process artifacts emerged: (a) Wave 8.1 fanout omnibus accumulated 4 checkpoint commits due to stop-hook firing on untracked files written by async subagents whose notifications hadn't yet arrived — final state is correct but the checkpoint history is busier than ideal; (b) BOTH U-C and D7-U-1 volunteered for H-1 stable-ID lettering convention (auto-008 said "recommend ONE" but didn't forbid both); their adoption mechanisms are complementary (anchor.kind enum vs FC envelope IDs).

## Part 2 — What went well

1. **Pre-folded auto-007 audit-trail amendments at auto-008 Round 1.** The brief acknowledged the 5 Phase-7 retro AGENTS-MD-* rules were NOT adopted into canonical AGENTS.md (user election) and applied the patterns informally. Round-1 regulator did not surface any of the 9 audit-trail defects that auto-007's regulator surfaced — the pre-fold worked. Time saved: ~1 hour of Round-2 amendments.

2. **Pre-authored Phase-7 cite-obligation propagation table in auto-008.** Subagents read their row of the table rather than deriving from the Phase-7 aggregation matrix. 9 subagents × ~10 min derivation saved = ~90 min cumulative. All 9 honored their cite obligations verbatim per [`audit-falsification-designer.md`](../architectures/v3/lean-evals/audit-falsification-designer.md).

3. **Lead-agent falsifier cross-check artifact (R5 #1 amendment).** Wrote `cross-check-falsifier.md` recording 0 rewrite-§3 verdicts → Phase-8-followup deferral NOT FIRED → Wave-8.2 lead-agent-authored path active. The artifact closed the recovery hole the on-call-engineer reviewer (R5) flagged in Round 2.

4. **R6 cross-mandate-attacker reviewer angle.** This angle was load-bearing for finding the DEC-1.a falsifying-pattern vs file-model structural mismatch. Adding "cross-mandate attacker" to the adversarial-reviewer-angles catalog as a Round-2 default for any multi-mandate hypothesis brief would catch this class of defect earlier.

5. **All 9 Wave-8.1 subagents within tier without re-author cycles.** The auto-008 tier-table (Light 5000-6500 / Heavy 5500-7200, calibrated from Phase-7 actuals) produced 9 in-tier briefs on the first dispatch. Median Light 5188; median Heavy 5714 — substantially closer to the floor than Phase-7's actuals.

## Part 3 — What didn't go well

1. **Stop-hook + async-subagent coordination produced multi-checkpoint Wave-8.1 fanout commit history.** When async subagents wrote files between stop-hook fires, the lead-agent committed snapshots that subagents then revised after notification. Result: 4 commits on the fanout branch where 1 was ideal. Final state correct; history busier.

2. **GF-M exemplar landed under Light tier floor (4434 vs 5000-6500).** Acknowledged in self-check section. Wave 8.1 subagents targeted their own tier (all 9 within tier) so the exemplar's under-tier didn't propagate. But a stricter pre-fanout gate could have caught this.

3. **5 Phase-7 retro AGENTS-MD-* rules not adopted into canonical AGENTS.md** (user election). Brief cites them by retrospective-directory path. Future-agent grep against canonical `AGENTS.md` will not find them. Honest-acknowledgement + canonical-grep-guidance recorded per R5 #4 amendment.

4. **H-1 stable-ID lettering: BOTH U-C and D7-U-1 volunteered** when auto-008 said "recommend ONE". Their adoption mechanisms are complementary but the over-adoption was not anticipated by the historian's recommendation. Non-defect; flagged for downstream-simulator-harness as a "choose one mapping or unify" decision.

5. **Three Round-1-amendment opportunities were left to Round 2.** Round-1 surfaced: A.1→A.2 default flip (R1+R3 converged), tier-table recalibration (already pre-folded but not aggressively challenged at R1), and the partitioned-mandate structural defect (caught only at R2 by cross-mandate attacker — R1 reviewers did not catch it). A more aggressive Round-1 mix (e.g., adding cross-mandate-attacker to R1 rather than R2) might have caught the R6 defect earlier.

## Part 4 — Lessons learned

| Lesson | Evidence | Generalizable? |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-mandate-attacker is load-bearing for multi-mandate hypothesis briefs | R6 in auto-008 R2 found partitioned-mandate structural defect; R1 reviewers did not | Yes — should be a default angle for any brief that posits a falsifying pattern across multiple mandate/category surfaces |
| Practitioner-relevance vs mechanical-rigor is a distinct axis from falsifier-discipline | Domain-practitioner audit's load-bearing finding (3 unified-attempts mechanically-sound but practitioner-thin) | Yes — applies to any benchmark/eval design where the falsifier's metric could be either substrate-emitted or practitioner-felt |
| Pre-commit falsifying patterns with canonical-primitive guards before reading observed data | Hypothesis-falsifier's R2 #4 guard: composed pattern only from auto-008-committed terms BEFORE reading 10 briefs | Yes — applies to any "name the falsifier in advance" discipline |
| Stop-hook may fire on async-subagent-written files | Wave 8.1 fanout 4 commits instead of 1 | Process artifact; consider stop-hook adjustment for unattended runs with async subagents |
| Tier-table calibrated from prior-phase actuals produces in-tier subagent output | All 9 Wave 8.1 subagents in tier first-try with no re-author cycles | Yes — re-use Phase-N actual medians + small headroom for Phase-N+1 tier-table |
