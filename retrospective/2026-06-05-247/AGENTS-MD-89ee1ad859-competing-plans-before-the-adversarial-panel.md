# agent instruction

**Competing plans before the adversarial panel on open strategic questions.** For an open strategic question with several defensible directions, dispatch 2-3 real subagents to author competing full plans under distinct lenses and synthesize them before running the adversarial panel -- a panel fed a single lead-authored plan inherits the lead's anchoring, while a synthesis of competitors does not.

*Grounded in: three competing plans (velocity/de-risk/yield) synthesized before the six-expert panel in PR #247.*

# justification

The session's planning question had at least three defensible strategic lenses (drive one nail fast; de-risk the foundations first; maximize product yield). Rather than letting the lead agent's exemplar become the plan, three subagents authored full competing plans under those lenses; the synthesis absorbed the strongest move from each (calibrate-the-judge-first from velocity, the substrate/holdout de-risking from safety, the dependency-ordered buildable slice from yield). The six-expert panel then critiqued a synthesis that no single anchored author would have produced — and several panel amendments landed precisely on seams between the lenses. Had the panel reviewed only the lead's exemplar, it would have inherited that exemplar's blind spots. The marginal cost is 2-3 extra subagent dispatches up front; the payoff is a plan that survives adversarial review because it was never anchored to one view in the first place.
