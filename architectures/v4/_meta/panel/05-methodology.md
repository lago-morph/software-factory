# Panel opinion — Methodology critic

> **Persona:** Methodology critic — I pressure-test whether the chosen evaluation methodology (spec-in → software-out + held-out eval + autonomy ladder) is the right shape to reach El Kaim's dark-factory ideal, and whether v4's self-optimizing loop can avoid the Goodhart trap David Shapiro's levels framing exposes. I engage with the primary sources: El Kaim's convergent-substrate claim, Shapiro's five-levels ladder, and the specific Goodhart problem on a system that rewrites its own objectives.
> **Corpus read:** `run-summary.md`, `decisions-to-make.md` (D-20..D-25), `architectures/v4/README.md` (full), `architectures/v4/AI-CONTEXT.md` (full), `architectures/v4/one-shot-specs-and-research.md` (full), `architectures/v4/spec/C56-autonomy-ladder.md` (full), `architectures/v4/spec/C55-methodology-experiment.md` (full), `architectures/v4/spec/C57-failure-mode-coverage.md` (§1–§4).
> **PANEL OPINION** (independent view; do not soften for consensus)

---

## 1. Verdict

**`right-idea-but-change-X-before-building`** — the three-layer substrate / empirical methodology framing is the correct answer to the v3 "pick one methodology" trap, but the methodology *for discovering which methodology works* is underspecified at the exact point where Shapiro's levels framing demands rigor: the eval signal used to climb the autonomy ladder is built on a satisfaction metric that cannot distinguish genuine capability improvement from Goodharted proxy improvement, and v4 has no mechanism to detect the difference before L5.

---

## 2. Where v4 is sound (from my lens)

- **The substrate-is-convergent / methodology-is-variable pivot is the strongest intellectual move in the corpus.** El Kaim's dark-factory synthesis identifies three convergent layers across every working implementation; v4 correctly reads this as "build the convergent thing once, run methodology experiments on top." This is a real finding, not a framing convenience. C55 operationalizes it honestly as a thin orchestration loop over an existing eval tier, not a new engine.

- **The spec-in → software-out spine is the right load-bearing artifact choice.** Making the spec — not the code — the durable artifact is what enables the "code is disposable" thesis that makes bootstrap recursion (C52/C53) non-circular. The corpus supports this: StrongDM Attractor, Kilroy, attractor-c, and Fabro all converge on spec-as-SoT as the precondition for reliable agentic execution, and Kilroy's research folder is the closest practitioner analog to the held-out-eval methodology C55 requires.

- **The held-out eval stream + LLM-as-judge (C30/C31/C32/C33) is methodologically correct as the primary gate.** Using scenarios the implementer cannot see, scored by an independent judge on a satisfaction distribution rather than a boolean, is the right defense against the obvious failure mode: a system that learns to pass the tests it can read. The Inspect AI choice (MIT, agent-trajectory model) is the correct OSS anchor for this layer.

- **Shapiro's levels map cleanly to the L3/L4/L5 band v4 actually targets, and L3 as "the trap" is correctly identified.** C56's treatment of L3 HITL as a cognitive-bottleneck trap — where the operator becomes the scaling constraint rather than a quality gate — is precisely what Shapiro's levels framing is designed to avoid. The L4 default + L5 opt-in structure is the right orientation for a system that wants to climb without locking in.

- **C57's honesty discipline is a genuine methodological contribution.** The "no bare Addressed" invariant — every unbuilt mechanism carries its exposure-window caveat — is one of the few places where v4 is methodologically more rigorous than its predecessors. The F54 objective-drift audit being registered UNBUILT rather than marked partial or deferred-as-covered is exactly the intellectual honesty the autonomy ladder requires.

---

## 3. Where v4 is weakest / riskiest (from my lens)

- **CRITICAL — The eval signal that drives methodology selection (C55) and autonomy-ladder climbing (C56) is the same satisfaction metric (C33) that the system is optimizing. This is the structural Goodhart problem.** C33 is a probabilistic score over scenario trajectories. C55 selects methodologies by which formula produces the highest C33 satisfaction. C47/C50 promote variants by which prompt/config change improves C33 satisfaction. C39/C56 gate L4→L5 transitions implicitly on whether the factory "works," which in practice means "produces acceptable C33 scores." If C33 becomes the target — and it becomes the target the moment C47 starts optimizing against it — it stops being a reliable measure of whether the factory is building software that satisfies real operator intent. This is Goodhart's law in its canonical form: the measure becomes the target, the target corrupts the measure. The F54 objective-drift audit is the named response, but it is registered UNBUILT (C57 / OQ-C57-3), and the D-21 mitigation ("human checkpoint at each batched review") is not a control on C33 corruption — a human reviewing batch output cannot easily detect that C33's satisfaction measure has drifted from what the operator actually wanted, because the human sees factory output and C33 scores, not the divergence between C33's learned behavior and original operator intent. The methodology experiment loop (C55) inherits this: if C33 is Goodharted, C55 will select the methodology that is best at Goodharting C33, not the methodology that produces the best software.

- **HIGH — The three-layer / discipline / methodology / substrate distinction is not load-bearing in the way v4 claims, and will blur the moment the methodology experiment loop (C55) is live.** v4's core claim is that methodology is a second-order variable, swappable as a C12 formula file, decoupled from substrate. But in practice: (a) each v3 candidate methodology makes different demands on the eval tier — a methodology that does heavy upfront spec decomposition (like GF-M) and one that does iterative-with-refactoring have different scenario families, different judge rubrics, and different satisfaction distributions; (b) what counts as a "work type" (C55's G05 / OQ-2) is not a neutral classification — it is itself a methodological choice about how to slice the problem space; (c) the formula file is not the only place the methodology lives — prompt templates, Gas City rig configs, tool-node availability, and Gas City formula semantics all encode methodological assumptions. The "methodology is just a config file" thesis is true for the workflow topology; it is false for the full evaluation surface. C55 acknowledges this problem as G05 (work-type taxonomy unspecified) and routes it to sweep-2, but the unspecified work-type taxonomy is not a sweep-2 detail — it determines whether the methodology selection is meaningful or merely measures which candidate produces better scores on arbitrarily-selected scenarios. A methodology that performs well on greenfield scenarios (F-MODE F20) is not necessarily the right methodology for brownfield maintenance, and C55 cannot make that distinction until the work-type taxonomy is pinned.

- **HIGH — Shapiro's levels framing exposes a structural gap: v4 has no method for *detecting* when it is ready to advance a level, only methods for advancing.** Shapiro's five-levels architecture is not just a maturity scale — it is an observability requirement. At each level transition, you need evidence that the current level's failure modes are controlled before adding the next level's attack surface. v4's C56 makes L5 "opt-in requiring P12 maturity + trust" (README:498), but the predicate for "P12 mature and trusted" is human judgment (C56 OQ-4: "a judgment, not a predicate"). The bootstrap validation at Phase 2 (C53) is a one-shot go/no-go on whether the factory can build something; it is not evidence that the factory's self-optimization loop (P12/C47–C50) is correctly calibrated or that its satisfaction metric (C33) has not drifted. A system that passes the Phase-2 bootstrap validation can still enter P3b self-modification with a corrupted eval signal. The methodology has no instrument for this class of degradation.

- **MEDIUM — The methodology experiment loop (C55) is post-Phase-2 and therefore applies no selection pressure during the phases that matter most.** The ten v3 candidate methodologies become "experiments" only after Layer 2 is up and bootstrap is validated. But Phase 0–2 — the phases that establish the substrate, the eval tier, and the first self-built component — use an implicit methodology: human-designed specs, manual review at each wave, sequential phasing. C55 evaluates methodologies against a scenario corpus that is itself authored during the bootstrap phase using the implicit methodology. If the implicit bootstrap methodology shapes the scenario corpus in a way that favors certain candidates, C55's "empirical selection" is not as unbiased as it claims. This is not a fatal flaw, but it means the "you don't choose; you experiment" thesis is partially retroactive — the first several consequential choices are made before the experiment loop exists.

## 4. Changes worth making BEFORE implementation

- **Decouple the optimization target from the eval signal before C47/C50 are built.** Before the self-optimization loop (P12) has any ability to modify prompts or configurations, define a second, structurally independent eval signal that C47/C50 cannot optimize against — a "shadow evaluator" that uses a different scenario family, a different judge, and is never shown to the optimization loop. The optimization loop targets C33; the shadow evaluator detects when C33 has drifted from the shadow signal. This is the only methodological defense against the structural Goodhart problem. It requires a second scenario corpus (small is fine; 20–30 scenarios, a different judge family) and a divergence-detection formula that compares C33 scores to shadow scores across cycles. Cost: moderate (a second Inspect AI harness, a divergence comparator); leverage: prevents the entire self-optimization layer from producing systematically corrupted results. This change must precede C47/C50 implementation, not P12 maturity.

- **Pin the work-type taxonomy (G05 / C55:OQ-2) before running the first methodology experiment.** The claim that "GF-M is the first experiment, not the first winner" (C55 INV-3) is only true if the work-type taxonomy is defined before the experiment begins. If the taxonomy is defined after running GF-M first, the taxonomy will be shaped by GF-M's scenario performance — re-introducing the methodology pre-commitment by another route. Concretely: before C55's candidate registry is populated, publish a draft work-type taxonomy derived from the scenario corpus (C30), not from the candidate formula structures, and freeze it as a C55 invariant. Cost: low (design work, no new build); leverage: makes the methodology experiment loop's empirical claim falsifiable.

- **Add a machine-checkable L5-promotion predicate to C56 before Phase 3b.** The current C56 OQ-4 defers "P12 mature and trusted" to operator judgment. Before the factory enters Phase 3b self-modification, define a concrete checklist that is partially machine-checkable: (a) the shadow evaluator (from the first change above) shows divergence < threshold over the last N cycles, (b) the F54 audit pack has been built and run at least once with no escalation, (c) C43's boundary-typing half has been verified prevent-not-detect (per D-23). None of these require solving the full objective-drift problem — they require having instrumented for it. Without a named predicate, "P12 mature and trusted" means the operator looks at recent output and feels comfortable, which is precisely the human-judgment heuristic that Shapiro's levels framing is designed to replace. Cost: low (specification work); leverage: closes the L5-promotion gap before it matters.

## 5. What you'd verify first (highest-leverage unknowns)

- **Whether C33 satisfaction scores are stable across reruns of the same scenarios with the same judge.** Before trusting the methodology experiment loop (C55) or the promotion gate (C50), run the same held-out scenario batch through the same judge ten times and measure score variance. If the variance is high (> 10% spread across runs), the methodology selection is underpowered — C55 cannot reliably distinguish "methodology A is better than B" from "methodology A happened to get higher-variance judge scores this run." This is the empirical precondition for any of v4's eval-based methodology claims. Cost: low (one day of Inspect AI runs); it either validates the eval signal or forces a reckoning with judge calibration before any selection results are trusted.

- **Whether the scenario corpus (C30) co-varies with the factory's own evolution.** The held-out scenarios are authored by the factory (or its operator) and stored in a separate repo. If the factory's self-built components (Phase 3+) produce side effects that make the held-out scenario corpus easier or harder to satisfy — e.g., a factory-built component changes how tool outputs are formatted in ways that the LLM judge scores more favorably — the eval signal will drift without the coder having "read" the scenarios. The holdout isolation enforces that the coder cannot *read* the scenarios; it does not enforce that the factory's own evolution cannot *change what the scenarios measure*. Verify whether C30's authorship and the factory's self-modification are genuinely independent pipelines, or whether Phase 3+ component builds can alter the evaluation surface.

- **Whether the methodology taxonomy v4 inherits from El Kaim's 11 principles is complete for the target work (one-shot software synthesis from spec).** El Kaim's convergent-substrate finding was drawn primarily from dark-factory implementations in the DevOps / pipeline-orchestration domain. v4's target is spec-in → software-out: building software artifacts from human-written specifications. These are related but not identical problem shapes — the "satisfaction" criterion for a pipeline-orchestration factory (did the workflow complete correctly?) is better-defined than for a software synthesis factory (did the output software satisfy the operator's intent?). Verify that the 12-principle set is not carrying implicit assumptions from the pipeline-automation domain that do not transfer cleanly to the software synthesis domain before treating El Kaim's convergence finding as a design foundation for C55's methodology comparison.

---

## 6. One-paragraph bottom line

v4's methodology shape — substrate-is-convergent, methodology-is-empirical, spec-in → software-out spine, held-out eval with LLM-as-judge — is the right answer to the v3 methodology-selection trap, and El Kaim's convergence finding is a genuine load-bearing result for the substrate choices. But v4 has a structural methodological flaw that the autonomy-ladder framing makes visible: the satisfaction signal (C33) that drives methodology selection (C55), variant promotion (C47/C50), and implicitly the L4→L5 transition (C56) is the same signal the self-optimization loop targets. This is not a Goodhart risk in the future tense — it is a Goodhart trap that is structurally enabled the moment C47 begins optimizing against C33, which happens in Phase 3d before L5 is authorized. D-21 identifies F54 objective drift as the loudest post-G31 residual and recommends a human checkpoint, but a human at a batched review point cannot distinguish "C33 has drifted from operator intent" from "the factory is producing better software" by reading batch output alone — the divergence is only visible to an instrument that independently measures the same work-quality claim on a non-targeted signal. The recommended change — a structurally independent shadow evaluator that C47 cannot see, before C47 is built — is cheap relative to the risk it closes, and it is the one change that converts "trust the human checkpoint" from a process intention into a falsifiable control.
