# P-29 — Policy mediator (declarative gates)

**Dispatch tier.** per-primitive (designed-system).
**Claimed by.** [U-A policy mediator](../tracks/unified-A.md#1-architecture-sketch), [D7-U-1 compounding gate (variant)](../bias-guards/phase-3/d7-blind-axis/d7-u-1-prohibit-interval-escrow.md#1-architecture-sketch).

## Contract restatement

A policy mediator is a substrate primitive that sits at a typed primitive boundary, takes a **structured input document** (the candidate write / transition / artifact-availability event plus the calling principal, the named target object's typed envelope, and the substrate context the policy may need to consult — e.g., upstream verdicts, prior-cycle handles), evaluates a **declarative policy expression written in a domain-specific policy language** (Rego, Cedar) against that input, and emits a **structured allow/deny verdict** (`allow: bool`, plus typed `reasons[]`, `obligations[]`, and `audit_envelope`). The verdict is *binding*: the calling boundary refuses to complete the write/transition unless `allow == true`. Policies are versioned, content-addressed, and human-readable — drift is diff-able and Patrol-monitorable. The mediator does not embed business logic; it executes whatever policy bundle the deployment loaded. The two-part discipline (declarative DSL + boundary enforcement) is the load-bearing property: gate behaviour cannot be amended by anyone who can write code into the calling primitive without also amending the policy bundle, which is itself version-controlled and reviewable independently.

## Construction path

**Primary path: Open Policy Agent (OPA) with Rego, run as a sidecar or in-process library, called at the boundary via the `opa eval` CLI or the Go/Rust embedded API.** OPA is a CNCF-graduated declarative policy engine; Rego is its DSL. **Integration sentence.** The boundary primitive serialises the candidate event to a JSON `input` document, the substrate keeps the Rego policy bundle plus any reference data in OPA's `data` tree, and the gate decision is the literal output of `opa eval --input event.json --data policy.rego --data refdata.json --query 'data.gate.allow'` (or the equivalent `rego.New(...).Eval(ctx)` call from an embedded host) — the Boolean (and the structured `data.gate.deny_reasons`) is what the boundary keys its commit-or-refuse decision on.

**Alternative path: Cedar (AWS-originated, open-sourced) with the Cedar DSL, called via the Cedar Rust/Java SDKs.** Cedar's policy model is finer-grained (per-action, per-resource, per-principal, with formally verified evaluation semantics) and its DSL is purpose-built for authorisation rather than general policy. **Integration sentence.** The boundary calls `Authorizer::is_authorized(&request, &policy_set, &entities)` with the candidate write expressed as a Cedar `Request{principal, action, resource, context}` and the typed envelope of the target object expressed as Cedar `Entity` values; the returned `Decision::Allow | Decision::Deny` plus the matched policy IDs is the verdict. Cedar's analyzer (`cedar-policy-validator`) additionally proves at policy-load time that no policy violates declared invariants — a property OPA matches only with extra schema discipline.

**Prior art.** OPA in Kubernetes admission control, Envoy ext_authz, Terraform plan-gating; Cedar in AWS Verified Permissions; both are battle-tested at production substrate boundaries.

## Per-variant notes (no same-vs-distinct verdict — Phase 4.2 question)

- **U-A policy mediator.** Gates writes to the typed-object store (P-28-U-A variant) and reads from the same store at interval-close time. The Rego policy expresses each `EscrowInterval`'s declared `policies` block (gate / log / sandbox / approval-gate / reflection-trigger / judge-diversity) as a set of *closure conditions*: an interval cannot close (and its output artifact cannot become available downstream) unless every declared policy slot has a satisfying record in the upstream input (judge-router verdict, sandbox-runtime attestation, reflection-trigger confirmation). The policy DSL design content is the **interval-policy schema** the mediator's Rego rules read — what counts as a satisfying record per slot, how `judge-diversity: different-family` is checked against the judge router's recorded provider family. Corpus-why: [unified-A §1 substrate primitive #2](../tracks/unified-A.md#1-architecture-sketch) ("Policy mediator. Reads `policies` from the interval, fires the gates and judges in order, refuses to close the interval unless all enforced policies passed. *Substrate, not methodology.*") and [unified-A §4 D-4](../tracks/unified-A.md#4-4-defaults-accepted-vs-challenged) (substrate-enforced holdout discipline at every boundary — F28 mitigation).

- **D7-U-1 compounding gate.** Gates *downstream artifact availability* on declared-FC-survival. Rego policy expresses the rule: artifact A is available as input to a future artifact B only if A's matching FalsificationCommitment in the P-28-D7 FC store has `verdict.outcome ∈ {survived, conditionally-survived-with-window}` AND (when `conditionally-survived-with-window`) the current cycle index is still inside the declared `survival-window`. The mediator's input document carries the candidate downstream artifact handle, the upstream artifact handle, and a pointer into the FC store; the Rego rules walk the FC ledger entries (loaded as `data.fc_ledger`) and return `allow` only on a survived-FC match. The policy DSL design content is the **FC-survival vocabulary** (what counts as a `survived` verdict, how `survival-window` expiry interacts with re-falsification triggers from the opposing-side router P-33). Corpus-why: [D7-U-1 §1 substrate primitive #3](../bias-guards/phase-3/d7-blind-axis/d7-u-1-prohibit-interval-escrow.md#1-architecture-sketch) ("Compounding gate. Refuses to make artifact A available to downstream artifact B unless A's declared FC has `outcome ∈ {survived, conditionally-survived-with-window}`. Structural replacement for voluntary review (F53) and D-4 holdout discipline at every artifact boundary.") and the F53 / F46 mitigation framing in [D7-U-1 §2 F-mode coverage](../bias-guards/phase-3/d7-blind-axis/d7-u-1-prohibit-interval-escrow.md#f-mode-coverage).

**Same-vs-distinct verdict deliberately not rendered.** Per the [scoping principle](../phase-3.4-decisions-resolved.md#scoping-principle-immutable-overrides-any-conflicting-framing-in-the-integration-brief) and the [auto-001 Round-2 dispatch constraint](../decisions/auto-001-phase-3.5-dispatch-shape.md#amendments-to-the-dispatch-brief-that-will-be-sent-to-subagents), whether U-A's policy mediator and D7-U-1's compounding gate are "the same primitive" with two policy bundles or "two distinct primitives that happen to share a construction tool" is a Phase 4.2 / methodology-matching question and is **not adjudicated here**. Each variant gets its own contract paragraph above; the construction tool (OPA + Rego, or Cedar) is genuinely shared, which is one piece of evidence Phase 4.2 will weigh — not a conclusion this sketch is permitted to draw.

## Corpus-why (cross-variant)

The corpus problem both variants address is **F53 (voluntary-discipline fragility)** ([failure-modes-v3.md §F53](../failure-modes-v3.md)) at the substrate layer: any gate whose enforcement depends on an operator (or an LLM) *choosing* to apply it under time pressure will be skipped exactly when it matters. A declarative policy mediator turns the gate into a structural property of the boundary — the calling primitive *cannot* complete the write without an `allow` verdict, regardless of operator intent. Adjacent F-modes: **F56 (guardrail-bypass under stress)** ([failure-modes-v3.md §F56](../failure-modes-v3.md)) — instruction-shaped guardrails fail; deterministic-policy-mediator gates do not. **F28 (holdout leakage)** ([failure-modes-v3.md §F28](../failure-modes-v3.md)) is mitigated by U-A's variant binding the holdout/builder separation into the closure rule. **F44 (lethal-trifecta production-scissors default)** is mitigated by both variants enforcing substrate-default-off semantics in policy.

## Research-grade-uncertainty flag

**None.** OPA + Rego and Cedar are production-grade, widely deployed, and well-documented; the construction-path question is uncontroversial. Per-variant policy-DSL design is non-trivial (especially D7-U-1's FC-survival vocabulary), but that is *designed-system* content, not research-grade uncertainty.

## Buildability verdict

**`designed-system`.** Construction tool is commodity (OPA / Cedar). The design content is the per-variant policy DSL: U-A's interval-policy closure rules and D7-U-1's FC-survival compounding rules. Both variants have viable, independently buildable construction paths today.
