## How I guarded against fitting the observed pattern (R2 #4 mandatory)

The hypothesis-falsifier names the DEC-1.a falsifying pattern AFTER reading the 10 finalized briefs. This is genuinely in-advance of any simulator run but is structurally tunable to the brief corpus. I used **two stacked guards** to constrain that tuning, and I am honest about what each one does and does not buy.

**Guard 1 — Canonical-primitive constraint (the dominant guard).** Per the R2 #4 amendment's third named option: I constrained my falsifier statement to use ONLY the canonical R2 #2 escape-hatch enumeration (out-of-mandate scope claim / scenario-skip / criterion-substitution, plus the R6 #5 structural rider for unified-attempts) AND the canonical R2 #3 + R6 #2 partitioned "pass cleanly" definition (for unified-attempts: ≥80% of `greenfield-mandate-scenarios` pass §2 AND ≥80% of `brownfield-mandate-scenarios` pass §2 AND §3 `falsifying-outcome` NOT triggered on any scenario). The pattern is composed of these canonical primitives plus an integer threshold over the 4 unified-attempt candidates. This is the strongest available guard because the canonical terms were committed in [auto-008](../decisions/auto-008-phase-8-dispatch-shape.md) — a document that was Round-2-finalized BEFORE any per-candidate brief was authored — so the primitives I am composing pre-date the brief corpus structurally.

**Guard 2 — Independence draft (secondary, partial).** Before reading the 10 briefs, I drafted the pattern skeleton purely from the DEC-1.a hypothesis text ("no methodology serves both mandates") + the auto-008 canonical terms: "if ≥K of the 4 unified-attempts pass cleanly per-bloc without any escape-hatch, the hypothesis is falsified". The 10-brief read fixed K (see §Rationale) but did NOT change the skeleton. I then verified applicability against the 10 briefs.

**What these guards do NOT buy.** The canonical-primitive constraint cannot prevent the K threshold from being silently tuned. I picked K=1 (any one unified-attempt passing cleanly suffices) because that is the strongest possible falsifier of "NO methodology serves both" — the universal-negation form of the hypothesis is mechanically falsified by a single witness. Picking K≥2 would have been weaker but the falsification-designer audit shows all 4 unified-attempt briefs frame their §3 falsifier such that EITHER bloc collapsing triggers the brief's own falsifying-outcome, so K=1 is also the value most likely to be NOT vacuously satisfied by the brief corpus. Honest framing: the threshold K=1 is dictated by the hypothesis's logical form (universal negation), not by the briefs I read.

**Limitations I want named.** The cross-candidate falsifier I name below is structurally compatible with the form the 10 briefs already use (mandate-asymmetric collapse on the unified-attempts' §3 falsifiers). This is unavoidable — a falsifier defined in canonical-primitive terms necessarily uses the same vocabulary the briefs use. The guard is that I cannot move K or invent a new primitive type to match observed patterns; I am restricted to the primitives committed in [auto-008 R2 #2 + R2 #3 + R6 #2 + R6 #5](../decisions/auto-008-phase-8-dispatch-shape.md#r2-2--3-dec-1a-falsifying-pattern-canonical-terms-committed-in-this-brief).

## The DEC-1.a falsifying result pattern (verbatim — Wave 8.2 evaluator-brief MUST quote this in §X)

> **DEC-1.a falsifying result pattern.** DEC-1.a ("no methodology serves both mandates") is falsified iff, when the 10 lean-evals execute, **≥1 unified-attempt candidate** (U-A / U-B / U-C / D7-U-1) **passes cleanly** under the canonical R2 #3 + R6 #2 partitioned "pass cleanly" definition for unified-attempts — i.e., ALL of: (a′) ≥80% of that candidate's `greenfield-mandate-scenarios` (§1 partition) pass the brief's §2 success criteria AND ≥80% of that candidate's `brownfield-mandate-scenarios` (§1 partition) pass the brief's §2 success criteria; (b) the candidate's brief §3 `falsifying-outcome` is NOT triggered on any scenario in either bloc — AND the lean-eval result for that candidate **invokes none of the 3 canonical escape-hatches** (R2 #2: out-of-mandate scope claim; scenario-skip mid-run; criterion-substitution) NOR the R6 #5 structural rider (a unified-attempt with <3 scenarios scored in either mandate-bloc fails by construction). One such witness suffices; the hypothesis is universal-negation. If 0 of 4 unified-attempts meet this conjunction, DEC-1.a is NOT falsified by Phase-8 evidence. Mandate-aligned candidates (GF-S/GF-M/GF-C/BF-S/BF-M/BF-L) are NOT admissible witnesses — they serve only one mandate by design and cannot falsify the hypothesis whatever their lean-eval result.

## Rationale (why this pattern not another)

**Why K=1 (one unified-attempt witness).** DEC-1.a is logically a universal negation: "no methodology in the candidate set serves both mandates". The clean falsifier of a universal negation is a single existence-witness. K=1 is the weakest sufficient condition for falsification and therefore the most generous to the alternative-hypothesis (some methodology does serve both); a K≥2 threshold would be falsifier-conservative in a direction that bleeds into hypothesis-confirmation by raising the bar. The Phase-7 §6.4 NEUTRAL stance requires symmetry: the falsifier must be as easy to trigger as the universal-negation logic admits.

**Why partitioned pass-cleanly, not mandate-blind.** Per [auto-008 R6 #2](../decisions/auto-008-phase-8-dispatch-shape.md#r2-2--3-dec-1a-falsifying-pattern-canonical-terms-committed-in-this-brief): a unified-attempt with 6 greenfield + 4 brownfield scenarios where 8/10 pass could pass a mandate-blind ≥80% gate even if every brownfield scenario fails, masking the "methodology serves one mandate not both" pattern DEC-1.a is designed to detect. Mandate-blind aggregation is exactly the data-hiding form the hypothesis warns against; only partitioned per-bloc verification produces evidence the hypothesis can be tested against.

**Why escape-hatch exclusion is load-bearing.** A unified-attempt could pass the partitioned quantitative gate by (i) declaring brownfield scenarios out-of-scope (R2 #2 hatch 1 — but the R6 #5 structural rider catches this: <3 scenarios in either bloc fails by construction); (ii) skipping mid-run scenarios on the bloc that would have failed (R2 #2 hatch 2); (iii) substituting an easier success criterion (R2 #2 hatch 3). Without explicit escape-hatch exclusion, "pass cleanly" is meaningless — the candidate could pass by changing the question. The 3-item enumeration is canonically committed in [auto-008](../decisions/auto-008-phase-8-dispatch-shape.md#r2-2--3-dec-1a-falsifying-pattern-canonical-terms-committed-in-this-brief), so my pattern uses it verbatim by reference.

**Why §3 falsifying-outcome non-trigger is the second conjunct.** A unified-attempt could pass §2 success criteria on ≥80% of both blocs and still trip its own §3 falsifying-outcome (e.g., U-A's "methodology-delta-promotion-rate ≤0 on either bloc" can fire even when other §2 criteria succeed). The §3 falsifier is the candidate's own distinctive load-bearing-claim falsifier; triggering it means the candidate's central wager is empirically wrong even if the surface-level scenario gate is met. The partitioned "pass cleanly" definition makes BOTH the quantitative gate AND the §3 falsifying-outcome NOT-triggered required.

**What would NOT be a sufficient falsifier.** (a) Any mandate-aligned candidate (GF-S/GF-M/etc.) passing its lean-eval — these candidates' briefs explicitly serve only one mandate (`mandate-scenario-split: {greenfield|brownfield: N, other: 0}`), so their pass status is silent on the hypothesis. (b) A unified-attempt passing only the mandate-blind ≥80% gate — masks the asymmetric-collapse pattern. (c) A unified-attempt passing the partitioned gate but invoking any escape-hatch — the pass is theatre. (d) Per-candidate §3 falsifiers being individually well-formed but no candidate clearing the full conjunction — this is what the 10 briefs being well-authored looks like; it is necessary but not sufficient for DEC-1.a falsification.

## Observed-but-not-falsifying patterns

The 10 briefs surface several patterns that look like DEC-1.a evidence but are NOT cross-candidate falsifiers. Naming them so Wave 8.2 evaluator-brief does not promote them by accident.

**Pattern A — per-candidate distinctive-wager falsifiers (all 4 unified-attempts).** Each unified-attempt's §3 falsifier targets the candidate's *distinctive* unified-mandate wager: U-A's mandate-asymmetric `kind: methodology-delta` promotion-rate (per-bloc); U-B's low-confidence L0/L1 inference + failure to gracefully degrade (brownfield-only check); U-C's dispatcher regime-distribution divergence + asymmetric pass-rate; D7-U-1's opposing-side-kind KL divergence between mandate-blocs. Each is mechanically auditable and well-formed against the 4-item rubric. **But each is a single-candidate falsifier** — triggering U-A's §3 falsifies U-A's claim; it does NOT falsify the cross-candidate "no methodology serves both" hypothesis unless ALL 4 unified-attempts also fail. The cross-candidate falsifier is the *converse*: not "some unified-attempt's distinctive wager fails" but "some unified-attempt's distinctive wager survives AND it passes partitioned pass-cleanly AND no escape-hatch fires".

**Pattern B — MCC ≤0.55 / detection-rate <80% (5 mandate-aligned briefs: GF-S, GF-M, GF-C, BF-M, BF-L).** Per [audit-falsification-designer §Cross-candidate summary](audit-falsification-designer.md#cross-candidate-summary): five mandate-aligned briefs use the Larbi-MCC-ceiling falsifier form. These are well-formed but **target single-mandate primitives** (P-15 contradiction detector ensemble, P-21 paraphrase divergence, P-17 substance-check, P-27 archaeological brief recall, P-13 maintenance loop drift detection). A mandate-aligned candidate triggering OR not-triggering its §3 falsifier is silent on DEC-1.a — these candidates are not even claimants on the cross-mandate question.

**Pattern C — BF-S's two-disjunction falsifier (P-25 bypass + F12/F33/F44/F56 trifecta).** Mechanically equivalent to the MCC family but with a different metric shape. Same caveat as Pattern B: BF-S is brownfield-only by design; cannot witness DEC-1.a either direction.

**Pattern D — `solutions/audit/<primitive-id>/<scenario-id>.json` uniform shape (all 10).** Path consistency is a quality signal on the brief corpus, not a DEC-1.a signal. Lead-agent cross-check via uniform-grep is auditability infrastructure.

**Pattern E — unified-attempt §3 falsifiers all framed in "mandate-asymmetric collapse" form.** All 4 unified-attempts target collapse-on-one-bloc-but-not-the-other. **This is structurally compatible with DEC-1.a being upheld** (if the briefs predict collapse-on-one-bloc accurately, the candidates trigger §3 and fail pass-cleanly), but it is ALSO structurally compatible with DEC-1.a being falsified (if the predicted collapses do NOT materialise and §3 is not triggered, the candidates may pass cleanly). The §3 framing does NOT predetermine the hypothesis outcome; it is the right shape for a Phase-8 falsifier on a unified-attempt because it tests the candidate's distinctive cross-mandate claim, and as the falsification-designer audit confirms, all 4 are mechanically auditable.
