# Phase-3.2 pre-mortem critique — greenfield draft

## §1 Persona stance

I am the post-mortem author standing in May 2027, one year after the v3 greenfield architecture went live on a real greenfield project. The factory was deployed. The factory failed. My job is not to enumerate which boxes were unchecked — the team checked the boxes. My job is to tell the actual story of how, despite a careful synthesis, a substrate so carefully designed it had nine numbered primitives, a Cold-Start Bench with EARS-discipline and INCOSE GtWR lint, and a graduation protocol with four numbered criteria — the factory still produced almost no shipped software, lost its operator's confidence by month 4, and was quietly shut down in month 9. The cascade did not start where the draft's risk register pointed. It started exactly where draft §5 dispatch notes told me to look: at the operator-intent-illiteracy seam named in [GF-C OQ-6](../../tracks/greenfield-cold-start-first.md) — and from there it touched everything.

## §2 The pre-mortem story

**Month 1.** The operator, a domain expert with a defensible product idea, sat down to author the Intent Crucible. They produced something — three Intent blocks in the El Kaim 9-field shape (ROBUST-G4). The `invariants` field was populated as mandated. But the invariants were *thin*: "users must be able to recover their data," "the system must scale," "we must comply with GDPR." Each invariant *passed* the deterministic GtWR/EARS lint (ROBUST-G6) because the lint is deterministic on *form* — R7/R8/R9/R26/R35 catch vague terms, escape clauses, hedging language. The invariants did not have hedging language. They had the deep ambiguity that F41 (Under-Defined-Intent Debt, greenfield-critical) names: *correct-shaped but thin*. The Council, family-diverse per ROBUST-G10, interrogated the blocks. It produced clarifying questions. The operator answered them with the same depth of thought they had brought to the original authoring — because the *Council can only interrogate at the resolution the operator can engage at*. This is the exposure GF-C OQ-6 named: "the operator can author *something* but not something rich enough for the Council to interrogate." The architecture had no detection for this. The lint passed. Three Intent blocks were committed to the version-controlled ledger.

**Month 2–3.** The bench was seeded per ROBUST-G5: 5–10 Kaner-style scenarios, each bound to ≥1 EARS criterion and ≥1 invariant. The scenarios were authored against thin invariants and so were themselves thin — they exercised the literal-text reading of each invariant. The cross-family paraphrase divergence detector (ROBUST-G8) returned green: paraphrasers *agreed* on what the thin scenarios meant, because thin scenarios are unambiguous *at the surface*. F37 was structurally invisible because the contradiction lived between the operator's tacit intent and the explicit spec — not between paraphrases of the explicit spec. By the end of month 3, the bench passed its saturation check and one work-unit-class (a `regression-fix` shape, since the project had begun to accumulate "code" in the form of small sandbox probes) graduated under DPG-4 GF-C's four criteria. The cross-model judge agreement-rate held above threshold because both judges saw the same thin spec.

**Month 4.** The first lights-out work-unit-class was running. It produced PRs against scenarios. Each PR passed its EARS acceptance criterion. The operator — now in the upstream/downstream role per ROBUST-G1 — sample-audited and felt something was wrong. The code was correct. It did what the spec said. It did not do what *they meant*. This is F41 Under-Defined-Intent Debt at its named worst, but with one twist the draft did not anticipate: the operator could not articulate the gap, because they would have to articulate the very intent richness whose absence had caused the failure in the first place. The cognitive-escrow surface (ROBUST-G14) was firing reflection prompts at the structural moments per Splitter Cluster-7. The operator was clicking through them. Unified-B OQ-PLEF-5 had named this exactly: "the *operator's response* to the escrow primitive (do they actually read the reflection question? articulate the success criterion?) is itself voluntary. F53 may have a stronger reading than PLEF addresses." It did. The EscrowSurface was substrate-primitive, but the *engagement* with it was voluntary discipline rebadged.

**Month 5–7.** Patrol (ROBUST-G11) was running against the operator-declared invariants — the slow layer per GF-S §1.S5. The invariants were the thin ones from month 1. Patrol reported no drift because none of the produced code violated any literal-text invariant. The operator began authoring "hot fix" Intent blocks to patch their tacit intent into the spec. Each hot-fix block re-entered the cold-start protocol (the §5 micro-cold-start commitment from GF-C). Each hot-fix block was thin in the same way. The work units clustered just below the graduation threshold for the *new* invariants (F47 Goodhart-on-Tokens recursed onto the eligibility classifier: the operator implicitly tuned what they wrote into invariants so that the bench would pass and the classifier would graduate the unit, because work that did not graduate accrued frustration). The graduated `regression-fix` class continued running lights-out against thin invariants, producing more thin-invariant-conforming code. F40 Last-Mile Drift — greenfield severity `critical`, explicitly unsolved per DPG-8 and GF-S §5.5 — closed the loop: nothing shipped. The agent-shaped middle (per Schillace Letter 9) ran continuously; the manual fit-and-finish tail required the operator to articulate exactly what they could not articulate.

**Month 9.** The factory had produced 400+ PRs. Zero had been released. The board, receiving the structured Caremark prong-1 reports the RSI-Declaration Ledger (ROBUST-G19) had been generating from cycle 1, asked the operator to justify continued spend. The operator could not. The factory was shut down. The audit was beautiful. No F-mode in the catalog had fired in a strict-detection sense. F25 (Design starvation) had not fired because work was happening. F1/F27 had not fired because cross-model judges agreed. F44 had not fired because production-scissors were off and no scissors had been needed. F43 had not fired because the board *was* getting reports. The architecture had been substrate-perfect and methodology-coherent and had still failed.

## §3 Specific architectural commitments that caused the failure

1. **ROBUST-G4 (operator-authored Intent block as the upstream-stable artifact) treats the operator's intent-authoring capability as load-bearing without scaffolding it.** GF-C OQ-6 named this as the track's biggest unresolved exposure; the synthesis carried it into §3 as a Phase-5/6/8 work item rather than promoting it to DECISIONS-PENDING. This is the proximate cause: the architecture assumes the operator *can* author what the Council can interrogate. The corpus warrant for that assumption is exactly followup/10-governance.md §1.2 BCG "intent thinking is the critical new competency" — i.e., the corpus says it is a *learned skill most operators currently lack*. The synthesis used the corpus's diagnosis as if it were a prerequisite.

2. **ROBUST-G6 (deterministic GtWR/EARS lint) was treated as an authoring-layer F51 mitigation because it is deterministic.** It is — for the failure modes it *detects*. It detects hedging, vague terms, escape clauses. It does not detect *thin-but-well-formed intent*. The draft's §1.2 framing ("not LLM-judge — explicit refusal of F51 at the authoring layer") is structurally honest about F51 but conceals a different Ashby-deficiency: a *deterministic* guard against a *form* failure mode does not address the *substance* failure mode (F41) it shares the authoring boundary with.

3. **ROBUST-G14 (substrate-triggered EscrowSurface) promotes Kahana's escrow interval to substrate primitive on a single-anchor corpus.** F-ANCHOR-2 HIGH and DPG-7 named the single-source promotion risk, and Unified-B OQ-PLEF-5 named the stronger reading of F53. The synthesis carried both as Phase-3.2 / Phase-3.4 concerns. In the failure scenario, F53 fires as predicted: the substrate *fires* the reflection prompt at the structural moment; the operator's *engagement* with it remains voluntary; under cognitive load the engagement decays to clicking through.

4. **DPG-4 graduation-criteria + DPG-3 eligibility classifier compose into F47 Goodhart-on-Tokens applied to the classifier.** Once the operator has any pressure to ship — board, time, cost — they will tune the inputs (invariants, bench) so the classifier graduates the unit. GF-S OQ-T3 ("Is the eligibility classifier itself an LLM-judge primitive? Does F51 recurse?") named the technical version; the operator-pressure version is the same recursion at the *human* layer. The draft does not name an operator-side detector for this.

The cascade is: (1) thin intent slips past (2) form-deterministic lint → bench seeded against thin intent → paraphrase divergence agrees because thin specs are surface-unambiguous → graduation criteria pass → (4) lights-out runs against thin spec → F40 closes the loop because the operator cannot articulate the gap → (3) substrate-fired reflection prompts click through under load → Patrol audits invariant-drift against the thin invariants and finds none.

## §4 What would have prevented it — concrete Phase-3.4 edits

- **Promote GF-C OQ-6 (operator-intent-illiteracy) to a new DECISIONS-PENDING item, e.g. DPG-10.** Frame: "Does the substrate provide an *intent-richness probe* — a substrate-resident detector that surfaces when the operator's authored intent block is form-correct but substance-thin, distinct from the deterministic GtWR lint?" The concrete next action: a Phase-5 wave-1 ADR titled "Intent-richness probe vs. operator-discipline alone."

- **Strengthen ROBUST-G6 with an explicit *form vs. substance* caveat.** The deterministic lint addresses F38 (form-level vocabulary debt) and contributes to F51 mitigation at the authoring layer. It *does not* address F41. Add a sentence: "F41 (Under-Defined-Intent Debt, greenfield-critical) is *not* addressed by GtWR/EARS lint and remains an authoring-layer open concern; see DPG-10."

- **Replace the F40 deferral in DPG-8 with an explicit shipping-rate tripwire.** The pre-mortem shows the cascade only becomes visible at month 9 (board cost question) because no in-cycle signal observes shipping-rate. A substrate-level tripwire — "if Cold-Start Bench graduation events accumulate at rate R while *released artifact* count grows at rate r << R, fire a Patrol-class escalation" — would surface the cascade in months 4–5 rather than month 9.

- **Add an operator-engagement instrumentation requirement to ROBUST-G14.** The EscrowSurface fires reflection prompts; the substrate should *measure operator engagement with the prompt* — time-on-prompt, character-count of operator response, semantic distance from prior responses. This is the substrate-side response to Unified-B OQ-PLEF-5's stronger reading of F53. Detection of click-through-style engagement is itself a typed event Patrol can audit.

The combined effect: the architecture detects month-1 thin-intent (DPG-10 probe), names month-2 click-through engagement (G14 instrumentation), surfaces month-5 shipping-rate divergence (DPG-8 tripwire) — three points where the cascade can be cut before month 9. The draft as written defends every individual F-mode in the catalog and still produces the failure, because the failure rides the seams between F38 / F41 / F47 / F53 / F40 that no single F-mode names.
